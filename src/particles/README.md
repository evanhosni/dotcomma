# Particles

## How it works

THE PARTICLE BASE: mass, stateless, LOOPING GPU particles any system can draw. Nothing is simulated on the CPU; a frame costs one clock uniform. Its two members are DRESSING features in [objects/dressing/particles/](../objects/dressing/particles/), each a `createParticleDressing({…})` spec ([particleDressing.tsx](../objects/dressing/particles/particleDressing.tsx): the emitter enumerator and its args, the default render distance, and one or more LAYERS drawn from every emitter — each a look, an emit function, a max travel and a render order; one material and one mesh per layer per chunk — the mount check, the materials, the chunk lifecycle and the clock are the factory's): the white water of every river fall (`RiverSplashes.tsx`: a SPRAY layer and a MIST layer from the same emitters) and the bubbles rising off the floor of every water biome (`LakeBubbles.tsx`), each mounted ONCE under `<Domain>`.

- **The shader** ([particleMaterial.ts](particleMaterial.ts), `createParticleMaterial(look)`): every particle is an instance of a unit quad with its own `aOrigin`, `aVelocity`, `aLife`, `aPhase`, `aSize` and `aSeed`. The vertex stage computes its age as `(uTime − phase) mod life` (each loops forever, out of step with its neighbours; phases are spread over the life so the loop never pulses), its position as `origin + velocity·age + ½·gravity·age²` plus the look's wobble, and billboards the quad in VIEW space after the terrain's precision scheme (the chunk's wrapped origin + the local offset, quantized, projected through the CPU-resolved view-space origin — [world/shaders/chunkProjection.ts](../world/shaders/chunkProjection.ts)) and the curvature, so the billboard offset follows the curve. LOD in the shader by view distance: every particle within 120u, a share falling to 12% by 900u (each particle's seed decides, so the same ones stay), the survivors growing 2.2×; a dropped particle collapses off screen. The fragment stage draws a ragged soft disc (its edge wobbles by the seed) — a bright centre for spray, a bright rim with a highlight spot and a see-through middle for a bubble — faded in over the first tenth of the life and out over the last three tenths; MIST is a featureless haze densest at its middle, grown in over a quarter of its life and thinned away over the last half; all night-dimmed, dithered; transparent, no depth write, no tone mapping. A `ParticleLook` is colour, opacity, gravity, wobble and disc style; `SPLASH_LOOK` (opacity 0.7), `BUBBLE_LOOK` (0.6) and `MIST_LOOK` (0.22) are the three.
- **The clock**: `PARTICLE_CLOCK`, written by `tickParticles` from each member's `useFrame` (the same value twice costs nothing).
- **A chunk** ([particleChunk.ts](particleChunk.ts), `buildParticleChunk(writer, bounds, material, maxTravel, renderOrder)`): one `Mesh` over an `InstancedBufferGeometry` for every emitter of the 256u dressing chunk, collected through a `ParticleWriter`; origins relative to the chunk's centre (the mesh stands there), an explicit culling sphere padded by the farthest flight, warmed by `uploadOnFirstDraw`. The dressing base spawn-fades it as a chunk (`selfPatchedMaterial`). The render order is the member's: spray ABOVE the water sheet at `WATER_RENDER_ORDER + 1` (the sheet writes no depth: drawn before it, the spray vanished behind it), bubbles UNDER it at 0.
- **Placement** runs on the dressing worker, Three-free:
  - `riverSplashes` ([utils/workers/rivers/riverSplashes.ts](../utils/workers/rivers/riverSplashes.ts)): emitters in the plunge pool at the foot of every piece whose surface drops faster than `minGrade`, plus one BURST emitter (`burst: 1`) on the upstream water line of every waterfall boulder whose face stands wet ([waterfallRocks.ts](../utils/workers/rivers/waterfallRocks.ts) `getWaterfallBoulders`: 75% of the rock's mean half-width upstream of its centre, at the sheet there or the rock's top when the sheet runs over it, strength from the fall's grade, count from the rock's size over the same 0.5–1.6× range). The member's SPRAY layer seeds each emitter's particles from `seededStream("splash|x,z")`: scattered within 3u (1.5u for a burst), up 2–9u/s (3–11 for a burst), on downstream 0.5–4u/s (−0.5–2 for a burst: some falls back upstream), up to 3u/s sideways (4 for a burst) (all × 0.35 + 0.65 × the emitter's strength), gravity −14, life 0.4–1.6s (shorter for a burst), sizes 0.4–4u skewed small, max travel 14u. Its MIST layer (`seededStream("mist|x,z")`) adds 8% as many particles per emitter (at least one): within 2.5u, rising 0.4–1.2u/s and drifting downstream 0.3–1.2u/s, life 2.5–5s, sizes 8–20u (× 0.7–1.2 by strength), no gravity, a slow 0.6u wobble, max travel 30u (the drift plus a far-grown quad's half size), drawn over the spray (`WATER_RENDER_ORDER + 2`). Loads to 1000u (the shader thins it).
  - `lakeBubbles` ([utils/workers/lakeBubbles.ts](../utils/workers/lakeBubbles.ts)): a seeded jittered grid over the water biomes' cells, kept where the water is at least `minDepth` deep; bubbles rise 0.6–1.2u/s with the wobble, size 0.08–0.2u, start within 1.2u of their emitter and live `min(30, depth) / rise` seconds, so they pop at the surface; 160u render distance.
  - Both are chunk-owned by position and pinned deterministic in `riverSplashes.test.ts` / `lakeBubbles.test.ts`.

## How to add another

1. **A look**: a `ParticleLook` in `particleMaterial.ts`; a different disc shape is one more branch in the fragment shader.
2. **An emitter kind**: a Three-free enumerator under `utils/workers/` returning `{ x, y, z, count, … }` per emitter, chunk-owned by position (re-exported from `vertexCompute.ts`, one `DRESSING_ENUMERATORS` entry, a determinism test like `riverSplashes.test.ts`).
3. **The member**: a file like `RiverSplashes.tsx` — per layer an emit function pushing each emitter's particles into a `ParticleWriter` from its `seededStream`, and `createParticleDressing({ name, enumerator, emitters, layers: [{ look, emit, maxTravel, renderOrder }, …], defaultRenderDistance })`. A second look over the same emitters (the mist over the spray) is one more layer, not a second member: one enumerator call, one chunk lifecycle. Mount it once under `<Domain>` (a flag-driven or world-wide feature) or in a biome's `<Dressing>` (a biome-specific one).
