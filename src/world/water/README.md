# Water

## How it works

Water is a flat surface placed by the CPU; waves and shore fade are shader-only. Physics ignores it (the player's swimming reads the same surface through the height pipeline).

- **Lakes** ([../../utils/workers/lakes.ts](../../utils/workers/lakes.ts)): a biome with `water: { depth }`. Its level is the region base at the cell site minus `LAKE_SURFACE_BELOW_BASE`, blended across neighboring water cells (`lakeLevelAt`). The bed is built relative to that level (`lakeBowlHeight`: `SHORE_RISE` at the shore, `depth` under it one blend width in, with `lakeBedBumps` hummocks grown in by presence² so the shore is untouched); `shoreLift` raises nearby land so it never sits below the water.
- **Under the water:** the terrain carries `underwaterDepth` per vertex (`vUnderwaterDepth` in every biome frag — the lake's sand darkens with it), seaweed grows on the lake bed (an `underwater` foliage plant), the player swims ([player/README.md](../../player/README.md)) and the camera's view goes through the underwater pass ([vfx/underwater.ts](../../vfx/underwater.ts)). Physics still ignores the water itself.
- **The bed:** one shade for a lake's floor and a river's channel (`bedShade`, [../shaders/common.glsl](../shaders/common.glsl)): the sand darkens and cools with the depth of water over it (`vUnderwaterDepth`), so a channel runs into a lake with no seam; the lake biome also declares the floor's own sand as its `riverbed`, so the channel inside the basin samples the same texture as the floor around it (two sands under one shade still met in a visible step at the mouth).
- **Rivers** ([../../utils/workers/rivers/riverField.ts](../../utils/workers/rivers/riverField.ts)): the surface follows the terrain along the centerline minus `RIVER_SURFACE_BELOW`; `riverMouthShare` merges a river into lake water at its mouth, and `riverLakeMerge` sinks the land between a river and a lake close beside it under the lake (one water, no levee).
- **Spring caves** ([../../utils/workers/underground/springWater.ts](../../utils/workers/underground/springWater.ts)): a river that ends in a cave continues as the SAME sheet: under the mountain a vertex in the cave's water gets its level, depth and flow, and within `CAVE_WATER_MARGIN` of it the sheet dives (pipeline step 14) under that water (never under the mountain above).
- All produce `VertexResult.waterHeight` (`NaN` when no water is near) and `waterDepth` (> 0 where the sheet is drawn, < 0 a cave-water dive).
- **Mesh:** a chunk with water gets a child mesh on the same pooled grid (`ensureWaterMesh` in [../terrain/chunkObjects.ts](../terrain/chunkObjects.ts), filled by `writeWaterBuffers`), sharing the chunk's LOD fades and disposal (`releaseWater`). Wet vertices (`waterDepth` > 0) carry it; dry ones are pushed below the ground, or below a spring cave's water beside them.
- **Flow:** every water vertex carries `waterFlow` (`VertexResult.waterFlowX/Z`: the river's downhill direction scaled by its grade, 0 on a lake and over a mouth) — the terrain worker ships it with the water heights and depths and `writeWaterBuffers` writes the attribute.
- **Material** ([material.ts](material.ts)): one shared `getWaterMaterial()`, clock advanced by `tickWater()`. Vertex: three crossing swells beating faster and higher with the flow, curvature; fragment: three `worldFbm` normal octaves advected along the flow in two cross-faded phases (still water drifts; gentle, the finest octave weighing little), broad sparse pale streaks from the coarse octaves growing with the flow, fresnel, glint, depth color, alpha fade at the shore, night dim and dither. A FALL (a near-vertical sheet, by the screen-space normal) reads its noise across and down the fall, carried downward, and is drawn as aerated white water instead of by its depth.

## How to add another

1. In a biome's `spec.ts`, set `water: { depth }`, `joinable: true`, and usually `prohibitRoads: true` (template: `src/world/domains/overworld/regions/ocean/biomes/lake/`).

Tune: colors and fades in [material.ts](material.ts) (wavelengths must divide `WORLD_WRAP`); `LAKE_SURFACE_BELOW_BASE`, `SHORE_RISE` in `lakes.ts`; `RIVER_SURFACE_BELOW` in `rivers/riverNetwork.ts`.
