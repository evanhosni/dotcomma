# Water

## How it works

Water is a flat surface placed by the CPU; waves and shore fade are shader-only. Physics ignores it (the player's swimming reads the same surface through the height pipeline). This folder is the CLIENT side (the material); the pipeline is [utils/workers/water/](../../utils/workers/water), one folder per body and per feature of the base, and every decision behind a number is in [water/DECISIONS.md](../../utils/workers/water/DECISIONS.md).

```
utils/workers/water/
  index.ts      resolveWater: step 4, ONE composition (area → channel → walls → joins → owners), on a WaterContext
  params.ts     the base: <Water>, the body kinds and their registered hooks, <Quays/>, waterBodyDefaults, bodyOf
  material.ts   the colours: a mount's colour props, each body's resolved colours, writeWaterColors
  utils.ts      what the water reads of the world: the ground it hangs from (waterGroundAt, keepingVertex) and the roads
  types.ts, constants.ts
  channel/      THE carve every river and pond shares: channel, walls, <Banks/> relief; params.ts (WALL_DEFAULTS, <Banks/>)
  joins/        a river meeting a lake: mouth, the hold beside quays, merge, the widening toward lakes
  lakes/        AREA water: level, bowl, shore lift; params.ts (<Lake>: lakeOf, LAKE_DEFAULTS)
  rivers/       LINE water on its own grid; params.ts (<Rivers>: riversIn, RIVERS_DEFAULTS, the gap rules)
  ponds/        POINT water: the standalone ponds, every pond's kind, spot and disc; params.ts (<Pond>, PondSpec, POND_DEFAULTS)
  valleys/      the land RISES AWAY FROM WATER: every biome's additional height, 0 at the water, whole a blend away
  parts/        the parts' one scatter (bubbles, steam, an <Actor>), the base's falls (boulders, splashes); params.ts (THE ZONE RULE)
```

- **One hierarchy, like DOMAIN → REGION → BIOME.** `<Water fallGrade defaults>` under `<Domain>` is THE BASE of all water (one mesh, one material, one bed shade, one depth rule, what a FALL is, every body kind's domain-wide defaults; its children follow every water: `<WaterfallBoulders/>`, `<WaterfallSplashes/>`; no `<Water>`, no water — `DomainConfig.water`). The BODIES are `<Lake depth>` (a biome that IS water: area-shaped), `<Rivers chance>` (on a region or a biome: line-shaped, on their own grid) and `<Pond kind>` (on a region or a biome: point-shaped, several kinds per biome).
- **Every knob is a prop**, resolved biome → region → `<Water defaults={{ <kind>: … }}>` → the kind's default, and blended by zone weight wherever two biomes meet, so nothing steps at a wall (the table below). A `<Lake>`'s and a `<Pond>`'s params are resolved whole when the config is assembled (components/Water.tsx `waterBodyParamsOf`: the kind's defaults, the base's for the kind, the mount's own); a `<Rivers>` keeps only what it names and resolves prop by prop over its region's at run time (`rivers/params.ts` `riversIn`). The numbers left as constants are listed under "Not props".
- **ONE body contract** ([../components/Water.tsx](../components/Water.tsx) `declareWaterBody`): every body is declared on the same base, so every body takes the same children as its PARTS — `<Actor>`s, `<Banks/>` (a channel's: `<Rivers>` and `<Pond>` only), foliage (`<SeaweedField/>`), particles (`<WaterBubbles/>`, `<PondSteam/>`) — and lists the same way: `DomainConfig.waterBodies` holds `{ kind, id, regionId | biomeId, params, parts }` per mount (`waterBodyOf`), and each biome carries the slots of its own bodies and its region's it does not override (the same `id`: `SerializedBiome.waterBodies`, `params.ts` `bodyOf`). Every body's params carry its colours, and a disc-owning body's its walls, so the base reads them without naming a body. Every vertex names whose water — or bank — it is (`VertexResult.waterBody`, `waterBodyShare`, `waterBody2`, CPU only). A pond's whole disc has ONE owner (`FieldDisc.body`): its `<Pond>` — steam, actors, foliage and its banks all follow it. No body knows another, and the base knows none: what the base needs of a kind it cannot read off its params, the kind's own module registers (`params.ts` `registerWaterBodyKind`: a pond's `reachIn`).
- **ONE zone rule** (`parts/params.ts` `waterZoneOf`): a part stands where its body owns the vertex, in its ZONE by the water over the ground — `submerged` (≥ `SHALLOWS_DEPTH`, 0.9u), `shallows` (above it), `bank` (dry, still in the body's reach). A part picks it with `zone`; unset, its class's default (`WATER_ZONE_DEFAULTS`: actors the shallows, foliage and particles submerged). Particles and actors stand on ONE scatter (`parts/` `waterPartPointsIn`: an `<Actor>` in a body is `featurePlacement.ts`'s body branch, at its own `density`, so the server catalogs it like any feature-placed actor); foliage runs the same rule on its blade grid. A body kind that knows where its water lies narrows the scatter (its registered `reachIn`: a pond's boxes) — without it, a whole-biome scatter of beebles cost 34 ms a grass chunk; with it, 1.2. Particle mounts that agree on every knob share one enumeration (the particle base); a group whose knobs differ from the defaults seeds its scatter by them too, so overlapping bodies never emit the same points twice.
- **ONE composition** (`index.ts` `resolveWater`, `computeVertexData` step 4, on a `WaterContext` the pipeline fills: the vertex, its river field, and the quay and road input — `ownBanksWeight`, `riverBanksShare`, `streetCurbDip`, `freewayDistance`): AREA water (a lake's level, the shore lift), then CHANNEL water (the river field's carve, surface, bed and flow), its WALLS, then the JOINS (`joins/`: a mouth, the hold beside quays, a merge); the body slots come out of the same pass. The decks' clearance draws a river by the same rule (`drawnRiverWater`). The composition reads the rivers through [rivers/index.ts](../../utils/workers/water/rivers/index.ts), as `ponds/` and `parts/` do; the river modules import one another, and the pipeline's other systems (vertexCompute, roads/, bridges/, underground/, the components) import the river modules they read directly — they share the pipeline's import cycle.
- **Lakes** (`lakes/`): a biome with a `<Lake depth>` (`lakeOf(biome)`, the one lake test). Its level is the region base at the cell site minus `levelBelow`, blended across neighboring lake cells (`lakeLevelAt`), then brought DOWN under the natural ground of the land beside it (`landDeficitAt`: sampled on every lake|land wall, the water stands `levelBelowLand` under it there and eases back up at its `landGrade`), so the water meets low land at or under its ground and stays where it was along every higher shore. The bed is built relative to that level (`lakeBowlHeight`: `SHORE_RISE` at the shore, `depth` under it one blend width in, with `bedBumps` × `depth` hummocks grown in by presence² so the shore is untouched); `shoreLift` raises land within `shoreHold` (+ `shoreFade`) of the nearest lake so it never sits below the water; a river's surface eases onto its level over its `riverApproach`. No inter-city freeway runs along a lake's walls.
- **Under the water:** the terrain carries `underwaterDepth` per vertex (`vUnderwaterDepth` in every biome frag — the lake's sand darkens with it), seaweed grows on the lake bed (a `submerged` foliage plant mounted in the `<Lake>`, `submergedMinDepth`/`tipClearance`/`minCutScale` its props: [foliage/README.md](../../objects/foliage/README.md)), bubbles rise off it (`<WaterBubbles/>`), the player swims ([player/README.md](../../player/README.md)) and the camera's view goes through the underwater pass ([vfx/underwater.ts](../../vfx/underwater.ts)), tinted by the colours of the body the camera is in.
- **The bed:** one shade for a lake's floor and a river's channel (`bedShade`, [../shaders/common.glsl](../shaders/common.glsl)): the sand darkens and cools with the depth of water over it, so a channel runs into a lake with no seam; the lake biome also declares the floor's own sand as its `riverbed`, so the channel inside the basin samples the same texture as the floor around it. A biome whose riverbed declares `yieldSlope` gives way to its own ground on steep banks — in the shader by slope, and on the CPU by the bed limit (`rivers/bedLimit.ts`), so it never resumes past a steep step (the mountain: `[30, 40]`). In a road-bearing biome the bed yields to the road field's pavement (the shader's pavement mask: `roadBearingBiomeIds()`).
- **Ponds** (`ponds/`): a pond or a spring is a DISC of the river field (`FieldDisc`) — its factor-1 distance normalized by its waterline radius wobbled by noise on the unit circle (`blob`), its surface its level, its flow 0, its bed scaled by its kind's `depth`; the channel's carve does the rest. Its kind is a Three-free `PondSpec` (`domains/overworld/water/spec.ts`: `POND`, `SPRING`, `HOT_SPRING`; `maxSpread`, the standalone spot's steepness limit, default 0.35). Four modes place them, each a chance per opportunity (0–1): the river's END (`rivers/ponds.ts` `riverEndPondOf`: every natural end that is not a mouth — at a junction or at unbuilt land — rolls ONCE among the `riverEnd` chances of its biome's mounts; a miss, or no `<Pond riverEnd>` there, ends the river ROUND at its own width, the field's round cap closing it), `midRiver` on a stretch no steeper than its `calmGrade`, the SUMMIT of a gap the gorges left (`rivers/summits.ts`), and STANDALONE (one per 1000u cell of warped space, on the flattest clear spot of nine — `pondSite`, the one spot rule a summit's pond stands by too — away from lakes, rivers + the field's support, road-bearing biomes and freeways: `utils.ts` `pondTouchesRoad`, the one road check every pond makes, through the roads' pure distance queries). Every pond is at least its spot's `pondMinRadius` and, on a river, its `minRadiusOverRiver` × the river's half-width; its walls are its `<Pond>`'s. Steam (`<PondSteam/>`, a part of a channel body only) rises from a pond's DISCS; it is mounted on the hot springs alone. A region's `<Pond>` is every biome's there that does not mount the same kind; a biome's own mount REPLACES it whole (a lower or higher chance; `chance={0}` turns the kind off there), and the kinds' chances add up in JSX order (`pondKindAt`).
- **Valleys** (`valleys/`, `computeVertexData` step 3): the land RISES AWAY FROM WATER. Every biome has an ADDITIONAL HEIGHT (`additionalHeight`, negative allowed) and its BLEND distance (`additionalHeightBlend`), resolved biome → region → `<Terrain>` like `pondMinRadius` and blended between biomes by a compact kernel over the biome sites (reach max(blend/2, 1.5 cells)), so a mountain's 200 meets its neighbour's 20 over ~a blend, never on the 300u height blend. It is 0 on every FLOOR — a river's or pond's footprint (the field's `riverSample.gap` / `riverGapAt`, out to the walls' reach; a wider blend — the desert's — reads the rest off the far field's raw pieces on a 32u lattice, `farRiverGapAt`, faded in over the last 250u of that reach), a lake, and any biome whose own height is 0 (the city: its crisp plateau, quays, sewers and pads stay level), each of the last two by its voronoi territory (measured in `valleys/` from the floor sites' half-planes: the zone walls reach only the 5×5 grid) — and whole one blend away (smoothstep), so every body lies in a wide valley. It is part of the ground every height reader sees (the terrain, road grades, pads, decks, the server) but never the water's own probes (`waterGroundAt`, a river's road cap, the drawn surface): a surface and a level are read at the water's edge, where it is 0, and the field's pieces and discs are built from them. A far LOD (LOD4) reads its gap off the coarse far field (`rivers/field.ts farRiverFieldAt`).
- **Rivers** (`rivers/`, interpolated per vertex by `rivers/field.ts`): the surface hangs `WATER_BELOW_GROUND` under the lowest ground across the footprint; a junction's width is jittered × its `<Rivers width>` × the widening toward a region of lakes (`<Lake riverWidening riverWideningReach>`, by the region's share of lake); `riverMouthShare` merges a river into lake water at its mouth, and `riverLakeMerge` sinks the land between a river and a lake close beside it under the lake. Where a river may run and which gaps it crosses are its GAP RULES (`maxGrade`, `maxRidge`, `maxCrossGrade`, `minStretch`, `gapFill`, `maxCarve`, `shortGap`, `summitMaxGap`), judged per edge by the biome under its midpoint — the one its chance is rolled in — and at a junction by the stricter of the two edges. A biome mounting `<Quays/>` (the city) keeps its own banks: no walls, no bed limit, no merge there, a river beside it at a lake is held to the lake's level, and — inside the city's bespoke height branch, the only height code that keeps banks — a river's surface reads it at its floor. The water code asks for the mount, never for the biome; the road questions (who bears a belt, whose road field is pavement) go to `roadBearingBiomeIds()`.
- **Falls** (`parts/`): a river piece steeper than the base's `fallGrade` is a fall — its boulders (`<WaterfallBoulders spacing calmSpacing>`, drawn and collided as rocks, the server's obstacles following the same mount: [waterfall-boulders/](../../objects/dressing/waterfall-boulders/README.md)) and its white water (`<WaterfallSplashes spacing count mistShare>`, bursting on boulders only where they are mounted).
- **No water in caves.** A cave holds no water and no river runs into one (a river stopped by a mountain ends at its foot: a pond where one rolls, else round).
- All produce `VertexResult.waterHeight` (`NaN` when no water is near) and `waterDepth` (> 0 where the sheet is drawn).
- **Mesh:** a chunk with water gets a child mesh on the same pooled grid (`ensureWaterMesh` in [../terrain/chunkObjects.ts](../terrain/chunkObjects.ts), filled by `writeWaterBuffers`), sharing the chunk's LOD fades and disposal (`releaseWater`). Wet vertices (`waterDepth` > 0) carry it; dry ones are pushed below the ground, or below the water beside them.
- **Flow:** every water vertex carries `waterFlow` (`VertexResult.waterFlowX/Z`: the river's downhill direction scaled by its grade up to its `maxGrade`, 0 on a lake and over a mouth) — the terrain worker ships it with the water heights and depths and `writeWaterBuffers` writes the attribute.
- **Colour:** every body's water has its own `shallowColor`, `deepColor` and `deepDepth` (a pond's are its mount's, else its kind's), resolved like any prop — the body's, else its region's body of the same id, else the `<Water>` base's (defaults: the old sheet constants). The terrain worker writes the owning bodies' colours, blended by `waterBodyShare`, per vertex (`material.ts` `writeWaterColors` → the `waterShallow`/`waterDeep` attributes): ONE program, no variant per body. The underwater pass follows the same colours for the water the camera is in (the player's water probe carries its body slots): `LOOKS.water` is the defaults' look, each colour scaled by the body's against the defaults', the fog thickening as `deepDepth` shortens.
- **Material** ([material.ts](material.ts)): one shared `getWaterMaterial()`, clock advanced by `tickWater()`. Vertex: three crossing swells beating faster and higher with the flow, curvature; fragment: three `worldFbm` normal octaves advected along the flow in two cross-faded phases, broad sparse pale streaks growing with the flow, fresnel, glint, the body's colour by depth, alpha fade at the shore, night dim and dither. A FALL (a near-vertical sheet, by the screen-space normal) reads its noise across and down the fall and is drawn as aerated white water.

### The props

Every body prop below also takes a domain-wide default on the base: `<Water defaults={{ rivers: { maxCarve: 60 }, lake: { levelBelow: 2 }, pond: { wallGrade: 0.5 } }}>` (by body kind; under every mount's own, over the defaults in the table).

| prop | on | default | what it tunes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fallGrade` | `<Water>` | 0.25 | a piece steeper than this is a fall (boulders, white water) |
| `shallowColor`, `deepColor`, `deepDepth` | `<Water>`, `<Lake>`, `<Rivers>`, `<Pond>`, `PondSpec` | (0.22, 0.62, 0.62), (0.12, 0.2, 0.28), 14 | the water's colour, "#rrggbb" or an RGB triple; a body's falls back to its region's, then the base's (a pond's mount to its kind's) |
| `chance` | `<Rivers>` (region, biome) | region 1, biome its region's | the chance (0–1) per river-grid edge (by the biome under its midpoint) that it carries a river; 0 = dry |
| `width`, `depth` | `<Rivers>` | 1, 1 | × the domain's `<Terrain river>` shape: width per junction, depth blended by zone weight |
| `wallGrade`, `wallEase`, `wallReach` | `<Rivers>`, `<Pond>` | 0.6, 120, 1000 | the walls a channel cuts: rising `wallGrade` from its water band, easing into the ground from `wallEase` to `wallReach` past it (the field reads walls out to the widest reach mounted) |
| `additionalHeight`, `additionalHeightBlend` | `<Biome>`, `<Region>` (`<Terrain defaultAdditionalHeight defaultAdditionalHeightBlend>`) | 0, 500 (the overworld: 20, the ocean and the city 0, the mountain 200, the desert 5 over 2500) | how far the land rises above every water and every 0-height biome, and over how far from them (the valleys; negative sinks it) |
| `maxGrade`, `maxRidge`, `maxCrossGrade` | `<Rivers>` | 0.2, 6, 0.15 | HIGH ground no river is built on: a grade along it, a ridge under it (u), a hillside across it |
| `minStretch` | `<Rivers>` | 300 | a stretch shorter than this between two unbuilt ones is a pond blob, not built |
| `gapFill` | `<Rivers>` | 800 | a high gap up to this long with water both sides is filled, or cut as a gorge |
| `maxCarve`, `shortGap` | `<Rivers>` | 80, 500 | a gorge cuts a gap whose real ground rises less than `maxCarve` over its water line, or any gap up to `shortGap` (shorter ones are never climbed) |
| `summitMaxGap` | `<Rivers>` | 3000 | the longest gap the gorges left that a river climbs to a summit pond |
| `levelBelow`, `bedBumps`, `bedBumpScale` | `<Lake>` | 1.5, 3.5/26 of depth, 70 | its level under the base, its bed's hummocks |
| `shoreHold`, `shoreFade` | `<Lake>` | 160, 250 | how far land beside it is lifted onto its shore |
| `levelBelowLand`, `landGrade` | `<Lake>` | 1.5, 0.05 | how far under the natural ground of the land beside it the water stands, and how steeply it eases back up to its own level away from that land (its surface's steepest tilt; over at most 1500u) |

| `riverApproach` | `<Lake>` | 250 | a river's surface eases onto its level over this far from its wall (within `shoreHold` + `shoreFade`) |
| `riverEnd` | `<Pond>` (region, biome) | `chance` | the chance (0–1) per natural river END (not a mouth or a road's) that it is this kind's pond; the kinds' chances add up, and a miss ends the river round at its own width |
| `midRiver` | `<Pond>` | `chance` | the chance (0–1) per calm slot every 400u along a river |
| `summit` | `<Pond>` | `chance` | the chance (0–1) per gap a massif leaves between two waters that it is climbed to this kind's pond on top (`rivers/summits.ts`) |
| `standalone` | `<Pond>` | `chance` | the chance (0–1) per 1000u cell of the pond's biomes (1 = one per km²) |
| `chance` | `<Pond>` | 0 | every mode the mount does not name |
| `radius`, `blob` | `<Pond>` | the kind's | the kind's shape |
| `minRadiusOverRiver` | `<Pond>` | 2.1 | on a river its pond is at least this × the river's half-width in radius |
| `calmGrade` | `<Pond>` | 0.05 | a mid-river pond stands only on a stretch no steeper than this |
| `summitStream` | `<Pond>` | 0.5 | a river climbing to its summit pond narrows to this × its width there |

### Not props (constants, and why)

- **The opportunity grids** — the mid-river slot every `POND_MID_RIVER_SPACING` (400u, `rivers/constants.ts`) and the standalone cell `STANDALONE_CELL` (1000u, `ponds/index.ts`): both are rolled before any kind or biome is known, so they cannot be per mount; the chances are the lever. `STANDALONE_TRIES` (9) and `SUMMIT_SLIDE_DROP` (25u) are search effort, not a look.
- **`RIVER_MOUTH_REACH`** (160u): which pieces deep in a lake are built (they would be drawn under its water); network-internal and frozen with the map.
- **`RIVER_FIELD_READ`** (440u, `water/constants.ts`): the river field's support for everything but the walls and the valleys (the channel, the surface, the kernel, the quay's straight reads, a standalone pond's keep-off from rivers); past it to the widest `wallReach` mounted a point reads its walls and its distance alone (a valley blend wider than that reads its distance off the far field: Valleys).
- **`RIVER_POND_FACTOR`/`RIVER_POND_LENGTH`** (1.7, 100u), the widened end: they bound the field's widest footprint (`RIVER_SHAPED_MAX`: the tile pads, the disc cap, every keep-off reach), which a per-biome value would make config-dependent.
- **`RIVER_MAX_RELIEF`** (40u): coupled to the mountain's `<Noise offset>` (below).
- **`SHORE_RISE`** (1.5u) and **`WATER_BELOW_GROUND`** (2.5u), `water/constants.ts`: the one rim height every body's carve, the joins' ramps and the lake bowl share so their shores meet, and the river's sink deliberately deeper than it so a city quay (the city's own height code) stays above both.
- **The lake level's kernel** (`LAKE_LEVEL_REACH_CELLS`, 1.5 cells, `lakes/index.ts`): the 5×5 grid holds every cell within that reach; a wider or per-lake one would need a wider grid or an asymmetric kernel.

### Known couplings

- **The mountain's relief and the river rules.** The river network judges high ground on a cheap proxy that reads a biome's relief WITHOUT its dome; the mountain's `<Noise offset={70}>` keeps that relief past `RIVER_MAX_RELIEF` (40u) so its pieces read as rock. A flag saying "rivers treat this biome as rock" could not reproduce the proxy's fade toward the biome's edge, so it was left; changing the offset changes where rivers stop at a massif.
- **The road layer and the bridge rules** (`rivers/roadLayer.ts`): it judges deck-ability with the bridge builder's rules, re-exported through `utils.ts`, in its own code. `rivers/summits.ts` (no climb within a road's reach) and `rivers/surface.ts` (a road's cap on the water) read the road paths the same way.
- **`<Quays/>` and the city.** Only the bespoke city's height code keeps banks, so the mount's floor rule acts inside that branch alone (`vertexCompute.ts` `waterTerrainAt`), and the quay reads beside its wall follow the city's walls (`cityWallsOf`).

## How to add another

1. **A lake-type biome:** mount `<Lake depth={…}>` in its `<Biome>` (template: `src/world/domains/overworld/regions/ocean/biomes/lake/`), `joinable` so neighbouring lake cells read as one water. No freeway runs along it. Put its parts inside: `<Lake depth={26}><SeaweedField/><WaterBubbles/></Lake>`.
2. **Rivers:** `<Rivers chance>` in a region (the chance an edge carries one; none = no rivers), or in a biome to override it prop by prop (`chance={0}` = dry). How they END is a `<Pond riverEnd>` in the same region or biome: none, or a low chance, and most end round (the desert: 0.15). Where they may run is the gap rules, on the same mounts or domain-wide on `<Water defaults={{ rivers }}>`.
3. **A pond kind:** a `PondSpec` in `domains/overworld/water/spec.ts` (`radius` range, `depth` × the river's, `blob`, `maxSpread`, its colours); a mount is `<Pond kind={POND} standalone={0.5}>…parts…</Pond>` in a biome (or a region: every biome there that does not mount that kind itself). `standalone` 1 is one per km² of its biomes, before the keep-offs. Keep `radius × (1 + blob.amount)` under 163u (the field's widest footprint; it is clamped). Every pond is at least its spot's `pondMinRadius` (`<Biome>`, else `<Region>`, else `<Terrain defaultPondMinRadius>`, 55; the mountain 75).
4. **A part of a body:** mount it inside the body, with `zone` where its class's default is wrong: an `<Actor density>`, a foliage plant (it switches to the water mode by itself), a particle member with `within: "waterBody"` (or `"channelBody"`; its enumerator takes `bodies` with their zones, its knobs are props), `<Banks/>` (a channel's bank relief). A part of every water goes in `<Water>` (`within: "water"`; its params reach the pipeline as `DomainConfig.water.parts`, `waterBasePart`).
5. **A body type:** its declaration on `declareWaterBody` (`components/Water.tsx`, with its `defaults` if it resolves whole), its kind in `WATER_BODY_KINDS`, its own folder with a `params.ts`, its water in `resolveWater` naming its slot; its params carrying its colours (and its walls, if it owns discs); a `reachIn` registered with `registerWaterBodyKind` if it is small in its biomes.

Tune: the sheet's waves, foam and fades in [material.ts](material.ts) (wavelengths must divide `WORLD_WRAP`); its colours are props (above); everything in "Not props" is a constant for the reason given there.
