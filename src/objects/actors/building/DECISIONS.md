# Procedural buildings — decisions

> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md (Oct 2026) to keep it under the context limit. Section names quoted in the text ("Blending", "Rivers", …) refer to the other decision docs listed in CLAUDE.md under "Where the docs are".

**Procedural buildings** (`objects/actors/building/`; the pipeline, distances and knobs are in its README)
1. `src/objects/actors/building/` — `<Building>` generates a deterministic building from a seed (default: spawn coordinates), interior-first: rooms-per-floor × floors set the interior area, which sets the exterior footprint and height (so the shell realistically wraps the inside). Exterior: 4–8-sided masses — boxy slabs or rotated faceted canisters — grayscale segments with occasional accent colors, lips/bulges, gentle lean, scattered oval/skewed-quad windows, rooftop caps + crooked pipes; colors baked as vertex colors so all buildings share one material. Night window lights: each building has `windowLightChance` (0–1 fraction of windows that light per night; default 0.6, 0 = never) and `windowLightIntensity` (emissive strength of lit glass, default 1.4) — a shader patch on the shared exterior material (per-vertex vec3 `aWindow` = stable per-window random + chance + intensity, global uniforms driven by `getWindowLightsProgress()` and `getNightIndex()`) hashes each window against the per-night seed, so a DIFFERENT subset pops on sporadically over LIGHTS_TRANSITION_DURATION_MS each nightfall (off at dawn the same way), washing the glass yellowish with an emissive glow. No extra draw calls or light objects.
2. Exterior and interior are ONE building (the interior MESH is built on the first close approach, see 3): the shell has REAL THICKNESS — the outer surface leans/tapers/bulges freely, while the INNER surface is a straight PRISM of the interior polygon through the occupied floors (the wall cavity between them varies in thickness). A constant inner surface means BSP split walls extend EXACTLY to it at every story: a two-room floor is one dividing wall running exterior-to-exterior, never an enclosed room-within-a-room (wall ends on the BSP domain boundary are stretched to the polygon via `ringSpanAt`, tucked into the cavity). Ramps: EACH story gap gets its own independently placed straight-run ramp (random spot + axis/direction per gap, so floor 1→2 differs from 2→3), sitting OPEN inside a normal room — stories generate in order, placing the gap's shaft first (clear of the arrival hole/landing from below; clear of exterior door zones on the ground floor), then BSP-ing the floor around it: split walls, pillars, doorways, light panels, and child slots all avoid the shaft footprint and the arrival rects, and a split wall never T-junctions into a perpendicular wall at its doorway. Door openings get jamb reveals closing the cavity, and doors auto-nudge along their edge away from wall ends that dead-end into the perimeter. The interior renders as a single merged vertex-colored UNLIT mesh (walls, polygon slabs with per-gap ramp holes, ramps, light panels) with a fixed wrap-lambert BAKED into the vertex colors at build time (light panels excluded, staying full-bright) so same-color surfaces still read as separate planes at zero runtime cost; interior colors derive from the exterior ground-segment color (floor darker, ceiling lighter) and are overridable via the `interiorColors` option. The plan generator clamps outer-shell rings through the occupied floors (apothem-corrected for polygons, including an inserted ring exactly at `interiorTop`) so the shell never cuts inward — shape runs free above the top floor. The shell carve, inner carve, and door leaf share the same ring edge + param.
3. Door mechanic: each opening holds a real hinged door leaf (closed = extra cuboid collider). Clicking it (screen-center raycast ≤ `DOOR_INTERACT_REACH` 6u, cursor grows on hover) swings it open locally as a prediction and sends `door:<i>`; the server toggles the replicated state for every registrant after checking the player is within 6 + 3u of THAT DOOR (its position in the same seeded plan the sealed hull comes from — `doorOffsetsFor`, `server/src/game/physics/buildings.ts`). Measuring from the building origin instead (its CENTER, 9–16u from every facade door, measured over 200 seeds) silently refused every door click from the 001a817 server-sync commit on: doors never reached other players. INTERIOR LIFECYCLE (Building.tsx): the first time the player comes within `INTERIOR_DISTANCE` (60u) the interior MESH is built (queued phases — walls, slabs+ramps, shading+panels, merge — `beginBuildingInteriorBuild` in `buildingAssets.ts`; the main build only makes the interior COLLIDER data, `buildInteriorColliders(plan)` in `interiorGeometry.ts`) and mounted together with the building's `children`; from then on it stays mounted with whatever state its children hold until the building DESPAWNS (the refcounted cache disposes it when the last mount releases) — walking away only hides it (beyond `LIVE_DISTANCE` 150u, where the matrices also freeze). This is deliberate groundwork for items/NPCs inside: enter, change things, leave, come back, and it is as you left it unless you walked far enough to despawn the building. Interior state never depends on colliders (still <120u real, hull beyond). DOORS are visible for the building's whole life: inside `LIVE_DISTANCE` the real clickable leaves, beyond it `farDoors.ts` — ONE InstancedMesh for every far door in the world (one draw call; rebased near the camera; no lamp glow, whose patch reads only `modelMatrix`), its instance angles following the replicated door state. MEASURED: 218 far instances matched their real leaves within 3e-5u; interior geometry in a city sprint 21.9MB (every mounted building, 304) → only the approached ones. (This replaced the old render-to-texture portal system, which has since been REMOVED from the codebase entirely.)
4. **COLLIDER LOD — a building is NEVER without a collider** (`proxyCollider.ts`); only the fidelity changes with distance. **detail** (<120u): the real colliders — shell trimesh, interior walls, slabs, ramps, closed door leaves; doors work, mounted as React children under `<RigidBody>`. **hull** (beyond): ONE convex hull over the shell's 2D SILHOUETTE extruded bottom-to-top (`buildProxyHullVertices`, cached per seed on the assets), which TRACKS THE FOOTPRINT so an NPC contacting it reads as touching the wall. There is no third tier and no distance gate of its own: the proxy exists exactly when `!collidersActive`, so the collider gate's hysteresis covers the only swap there is. Why NPCs need any of this: they live out to ~243u and used to walk straight through distant walls, and the real colliders can't be everywhere (two trimesh QBVH builds, several ms each, hence the base's one-building-per-50ms activation throttle). Rapier QuickHulls whatever it is handed on every mount, so the input is kept MINIMAL: the silhouette is computed here (Andrew's monotone chain, deterministic — every client must agree on a cached-per-seed hull) and Rapier gets ~14 points where every ring corner of every level was ~83, worst 133, measured over 40 seeds. The prism is deliberately slightly FATTER than a hull over the raw corners — where the shell tapers or leans it keeps the full silhouette at every height — so it still contains the shell everywhere, seals at least as well, and costs fewer faces. Roof caps and pipes are deliberately excluded — nothing walks on them and letting them widen the hull would block NPCs further out in the street. A single enclosing AABB CUBOID was tried INSTEAD of the hull and REMOVED: cheapest possible shape (nothing to construct), but a 45°-rotated square's AABB is ~1.41× its width, so NPCs stopped short of facades and big rotated buildings blocked the sidewalk. NON-DEGENERACY IS AN INVARIANT, not a nicety: a degenerate cloud makes `ColliderDesc.convexHull` return null and, with the hull as the only proxy shape, that building would be passable — the desc is therefore built BEFORE the rigid body so a null can't leave an empty body behind, and `proxyCollider.test.ts` asserts it (plus determinism, the point count, and the covering guarantee against every loft's ring corners within the hull's height band). The proxy is built IMPERATIVELY (`world.createRigidBody` + `ColliderDesc.convexHull`, via `createProxyCollider`), NOT as `<RigidBody>`/`<ConvexHullCollider>`: hundreds of extra Object3Ds would pay `compose()` per frame — the exact cost the actor's matrix freezing exists to avoid — and a React collider under a frozen (`matrixWorldAutoUpdate = false`) group has no current world matrix to read. The handle is solid from creation, so there is no frame between assets landing and the first distance check in which a building is passable. Being convex it has NO door, so it SEALS the building — right at range, and why it is swapped for the real colliders up close. Whatever is INSIDE when a sealed tier appears simply STOPS MOVING, and that is DELIBERATE — not a bug to fix: a character controller embedded in a convex shape has a blocking contact in every direction, so `computeColliderMovement` hands back `computedMovement()` ≈ 0 and the body writes the position it already had, while the mixer — driven by the state machine, which never touches physics — keeps the walk cycle playing on the spot (a beeble's idle short-circuit then skips the shape cast entirely once its machine settles). A beeble jogging in place inside a distant building is the system working. An explicit freeze system (`setFrozen` on the state machine + a paused mixer + a Rapier point query for "am I inside a hull") was built for this and REMOVED as redundant — containment already freezes position, which is the whole requirement. The one input that is NOT self-limiting is GRAVITY: if `computedGrounded()` reads false while embedded, the beeble's vertical velocity integrates with no terminal clamp (it has no equivalent of the player's near-ground fall clamp, and no analytic backstop to catch it), and a large downward sweep starting inside a shape is exactly the tunneling case — if an occupant ever drops out the bottom of a sealed building, clamp that first. PERFORMANCE, and what is actually KNOWN vs assumed: the proxy colliders own NO React state — the tier is driven from the base's `onFrame` through the handle, because a `setState` from the frame loop re-renders that Building and defeats `ActorPool`'s element-identity bailout (it hands back cached elements so unchanged buildings never reconcile), MEASURED as a 1–5 fps LOSS on its own, worse than the work it was gating. Two large reductions in hull work were then measured as NO change at all — an 85% cut in the number of live proxies (a 320u band, since removed) and a 6× cheaper point cloud (~14 points vs ~83, worst 133, over 40 seeds) — so the hull count is NOT a known cost and nothing here should be assumed to be one. Eliminated by reading the code, in case it comes up again: `<Physics>`'s per-frame sync only walks R3F-registered bodies and `forEachActiveRigidBody` (fixed bodies are never active); the rapier context memo (`[paused, step, debug, colliders, gravity]`) is stable in steady state, so `useRapier()` in every Building does not churn renders. The FLIP SIDE of that first fact is a hard invariant: r-t-r's frame loop runs `rigidBodyStates.forEach` over EVERY MOUNTED `<RigidBody>` COMPONENT every frame (`getRigidBody` + `isSleeping` wasm calls per body at minimum, a full translation/rotation → compose/decompose → lerp/slerp path for non-sleeping ones), so the mounted-component count must stay a handful. A "build once, keep the detail `<RigidBody>` mounted but disabled, prebuild inside a 220u ring" variant of the collider LOD was BUILT and REVERTED as a measured fps regression: it multiplied mounted bodies ~10-20× (plus a several-seconds one-build-per-50ms burst on entering the city). If build-once/reuse is ever wanted again it must be IMPERATIVE like the proxy (bodies rapier-side only, never React-registered), with only the doors' RigidBody left in React inside the gate. A devmode checkbox that flipped every proxy collider in the world in one frame existed for exactly this measurement and was REMOVED once the answer was in — proxies are always on. Note `physics debug` rebuilds line geometry for EVERY collider in the world each frame, so it is not a valid baseline for this.
5. Every randomization knob is a `BuildingAttributes` field with a seeded default (the list is in the building README); `shellHeightRange` is named so it can never be confused with the terrain-height spawn filter `heightRange`, which lives on the same descriptor.
6. Geometry is cached per seed in `buildingAssets.ts`; interior wall boxes double as cuboid colliders; the exterior render triangles double as the trimesh collider (door openings included).
7. **Width, houses, handles, interior brightness (Oct 2026).** WIDTH: every sized building is multiplied by `widthScale` (default [1, 1.5]) AFTER the ramp-shaft fit, one roll for both axes (a per-axis roll was built and then dropped at Evan's request), because widening only enlarges the BSP block and so cannot break the fit. Measured over 300 seeds: city buildings went from ≤ ~33u across to 20.5–39.5u, and skyscrapers to 24.7–39.4u. The spacing follows the widest shell: footprint 30 → 40 (building) and 36 → 48 (skyscraper); road setback 23 → 28 and 28 → 33; `MAX_FOOTPRINT_REACH` 24 → 36. Both footprint changes thin the city's COUNT (about (30/40)² for buildings) for bigger shells. HOUSES (`ROOF_STYLE.PITCHED`, `HOUSE_SPEC`, which replaced the grass biome's `grass-building`) are 1–2 stories on 3–5 sides. Their walls are the door band's own ring continued up, because `ringAt`'s lean clearance (0.9/apothem vs the band's 0.24 inset) would step the wall out into a ledge above the doors. Their roof is NOT a body loft, so the proxy hull and the flatten silhouette ignore it, the same as caps. The roof rise is measured to the polygon's APOTHEM: measured to the corners, a triangle's roof came out at twice the pitch. Triangle houses reach up to ~24u (a triangle inscribes rooms poorly), so houses keep the 40u footprint. INTERIOR COLORS: the interior is unlit and the wall color came straight from the exterior band, so a near-white band (0xd0d4d8, then ×1.3 for the ceiling) clipped to white. The derived wall is now clamped to sRGB luminance [0.28, 0.46] (a 0.2 floor left a black tower's rooms gloomy after the baked shading, headless check), and every surface (overrides included) to [0.1, 0.56]. The light panels are deliberately left full-bright. PROXY HULL: the new rolls produced three nearly collinear silhouette corners (a bend of ~0.07°), and float32 rounding flipped the middle one off the hull (`proxyCollider.test.ts` failed). `convexHull2D` now drops a corner standing < 1e-3u off the line past it.
8. **House aspect (Oct 2026).** `aspectRange` (depth ÷ width, rolled LOG-uniform so k and 1/k are equally likely) replaces the default aspect roll for any story count. Houses use [0.6, 1/0.6] because Evan found the defaults too equilateral and too square. The defaults are 0.8–1.25 for one story and 0.92–1.08 for more, the latter near-square for the ramp shaft. MEASURED over 400 house seeds, as the plan's long side ÷ its short side: median 1.05 → 1.28, 90th percentile 1.20 → 1.56, max 1.24 → 1.65. Two-story houses that kept their second floor went 210 → 198 (the grow loop absorbs the stretch). The max reach is unchanged at 24.1u, because the size cap applies to the longer half, so the 40u footprint still holds. The default path still makes exactly one roll, so city buildings did not reshuffle.
9. **Hip roof = straight skeleton (Oct 2026).** The first roof shrank the eave ring's half-width and half-depth by the same run. That is a true hip roof only on a rectangle. On a stretched triangle or pentagon (ellipse-inscribed corners, any rotation), the corners slid along the wrong paths. Each face became two non-coplanar triangles (a visible crease), the top collapsed to a sliver along the world x or z axis instead of the house's shape, and the overhang (radius + o/apothem) was uneven. Evan noticed it on triangle houses. The roof is now the eave's STRAIGHT SKELETON (`hipRoofRings`, convex only): every edge moves inward at unit speed, with a snapshot at each edge collapse, simultaneous collapses merged, and the point count kept so each face is lofted ring to ring. Faces are planar at ONE pitch, a triangle peaks at its incenter, and a rectangle ridges along its long axis. The eave is `offsetRing(wall, −overhang)`, so every wall gets the same overhang. Collapsed edges leave coincident points, and `emitLoft` drops their zero-area triangles so the trimesh collider gets none. Pinned by `hipRoof.test.ts`: one slope across every face of 150 houses, equal overhang, incenter apex, ridge length, and offset distance. Checked in headless screenshots of 3-, 4- and 5-sided houses.
10. **Taller floors, steeper ramps, contrasting doors (Oct 2026).** Ceilings, exterior doors and interior doorways all went up 20% at Evan's request (ceiling 6 → 7.2 and the house's 4.6 → 5.52; door 3.2 → 3.84; doorway 4.2 → 5.04, then set to the exterior door height on request). Shells grow with them because every shell height is built from `storyHeight`; skyscrapers keep their 70–115u shells. Ramps keep the old 9.45u run and get STEEPER rather than longer (`RAMP_RUN_FACTOR` 1.5 → 1.26, ≈34° → ≈38.4°; the fallback is 1.25 → 1.22, ≈39.3°, under the 40° slide angle). This is Evan's choice, made knowing the player's uphill speed fades from 25° (≈60% at 34°, ≈27% at 38.4°); exempting ramps from that fade was at first declined; they were then exempted on Evan's request (`RampCuboid` → `useFullSpeedSlope`, physics/README.md), so a ramp is now climbed at full speed. Measured over 600 seeds per spec: city building and skyscraper story counts are unchanged, and 11 of 307 two-story houses fell back to one story. DOORS: 40% of doors took a shell color, sometimes the band's own, and browns and grays could match a dark band too (a near-invisible door, screenshot). `contrastDoor` now moves the door's luminance at least 0.15 from both the band and the interior wall, lighter where there is room, else darker. It draws no extra randomness, so no plan reshuffles. Pinned by `generatePlan.test.ts`. LIGHT PANELS: placement tested only the panel CENTER, 1u inside the interior ring, but a panel is 1.4 × 2.8u, so its long side reached 0.4u past the inner surface, through a house's 0.24u wall (lit corners outside under the eaves, screenshot). All four corners must now sit 0.3u inside (`panelClearOfShell`), tested after the panel's rng draw so no plan reshuffles. Pinned by the same test file.
11. **Light fixtures, house windows (Oct 2026).** `lightType` (`LIGHT_TYPE`, default PANEL) picks the ceiling fixture. PANEL is the fluorescent 1.4 × 2.8u grid across the ceiling. DOME (houses) puts 1–3 domes per room, one per 60u² of room and spaced along its long axis; it skips any over a ramp hole and draws no randomness. Flat-colored and unlit, a dome read as a disc in headless screenshots, so its color fades to 0.7 at the rim. Houses light 0.3 of their windows at night, half the city default of 0.6 (Evan's call).
