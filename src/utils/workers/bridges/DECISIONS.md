## Inter-city freeways and bridges

**Inter-city freeways and bridges** (`getNetwork` + `computeVertexData` step 5; paint in `combineBiomeMaterials`; `objects/dressing/bridges/`). FREEWAYS ARE A SET OF WALLS, walked out into polylines (`FreewayRun`: warped points + cumulative arc length + dash phase + bounding box): (1) adjacent city cells form one CITY (union-find over city|city walls — one road per pair of neighboring cities, never one per cell pair: per-cell straight runs gave three near-parallel roads out of one city and one skimming its belt), and every pair of cities within `FREEWAY_LINK_CELLS` (4) gets the SHORTEST PATH (binary-heap Dijkstra, ties broken by junction position) over walkable walls (no city, no water and no `prohibitRoads` biome on either side — the lake sets it; rivers live on their own grid and cross roads freely, see the rivers paragraph) from a junction on one city's boundary to one on the other's — a third city's junction is forbidden (it relays), a path longer than `FREEWAY_PATH_MAX_STRETCH` (1.7) × the straight distance is dropped, and so is any path touching the window's untrusted border; (2) GAP HOPS: every walkable chain of ≤ `FREEWAY_GAP_WALLS` (2) walls and ≤ `FREEWAY_GAP_LENGTH` (450u) between the boundaries of two DIFFERENT city cells with no city junction in between — the short grass gaps between lobes of one city (a screenshot circled three). Shared walls merge, hubs form where chains meet. Every run starts at a wall junction, i.e. a corner of the BELT, which is CENTERED ON THE WALL: its inner half is the city's rim, its outer half rides the neighbor biome — grass, dunes or a LAKE (a causeway on the city's grade above the water) — so the two roads meet as one network node with no gap and no material change (the inset belt whose outer lane edge sat on the wall met every run across a strip of grass). Step 5, for every non-city vertex: the nearest of {run, city wall} (`nearestFreewayRun`, `findNearestCityWall`) is the ROAD; within `freewayWidth + FREEWAY_GRADE_RAMP` the terrain rides the GRADE — ONE per centerline point, a pure function of the road (`freewayGradeAt`, under the VERTEX's own zone weights the two halves of a run lying on a biome/region wall rose to different grades, a crease down the middle): the terrain at the centerline point with ITS OWN wall pass (`terrainOnlyAt`, the city at its rim grade), sampled on an 8u lattice per segment (cached, exact-keyed — a `toFixed` key made it order-dependent), smoothed along the run over 5 taps (reflected past its ends so two runs agree at a hub), blended over nearby segments by distance and over one run's legs by ARC, the warp's shear corrected so the cross-section is square in the world, a leg the vertex lies PAST the end of giving way continuously to the leg it meets (`gradeShares`: dropping it outright, and counting the first-listed leg on the outer side of a bend, flipped legs on lines through the vertex — 0.05–1.14u steps beside every bend and belt corner) — flat across, dipped `curbHeight` inside `roadWidth` exactly like the city's field, ramping back over 10u EXCEPT where the road YIELDS to a river — across the whole footprint for the belt's outer half (its inner half is the city's, which the quay yields there), only within `runRiverYield()` (the water band + 6) for a run, blended where the two meet — so the water stays open under the road and a run grazing a bank or a pond stays a road; the ground under an actual deck is cut by step 7 (see BRIDGES below). `distanceToRoadCenter` (normalized street units, 99999 off-road — it no longer starts at the biome-boundary distance) is written continuously out to `FREEWAY_FIELD_REACH` (70u): the shader's corridor mask interpolates it per vertex, and a 99999 beside a 9 aliased the corridor's edge into the triangle grid. Inside that yield zone the field is PUSHED past the pavement band (sand under the deck), a run's push LINEAR from its yield line over `ROAD_RIVER_RAMP` (16) units (`13·(yield − d)/ramp`, capped 40) and smooth-maxed into the field, the belt's outer half giving way with the SAME function as the city's quay river side, the two blended by share — a hard jump at the footprint's edge aliased into a sawtooth asphalt/sand edge along the LOD triangles (screenshots), and two different give-way rules meeting within a few units left pointed tips; the ramp lies inside what a deck covers. `distanceToFreewayCenter`/`freewayAlong` (lane paint) are only set where the yield mask is 1 and NOT within `freewayWidth + FREEWAY_MERGE_CLEAR` of both a run and a city wall — the MERGE MOUTH, blanked on the city side too, so the belt's lane line reads as branching off rather than running through. PAINT: the terrain shader calls the CITY'S OWN `city_frag` wherever `vDistanceToRoadCenter < 9.5` (a corridor mask over 8–9.5 street units = the curb strip's outer edge), mixed over the finished biome mix — so a run, the belt's outer half and the city's arterials are one asphalt, curb and lane paint (a separate `freewaySurface` helper was REJECTED: a different material, and it faded with the grass's presence beside the city). The corridor is deliberately NOT gated on being outside the city: on the wall the 2u material feather mixed the neighbor's ground into the belt's centerline as a green line. Spawn filters see roads through the normalized field (buildings ≥ 23; grass blades skip `roadDistanceRange` < 8.5); the belt's field recovers from `CITY_BELT_RECOVER_NORM` (9.5, one curb strip past its asphalt) so blocks and buildings stand near it (as a partner in every rim chamfer the squashed distance ate every block corner beside it into plaza). BRIDGES are DRESSING (`Bridges.tsx`, spec in `bridgeSpec.ts`, material in `deckMaterial.ts`; enumerator `getFreewayBridges`, the `bridges` entry of the dressing enumerator table, which skips the city probe; the header of `utils/workers/bridges/freewayBridges.ts` is the design in one screen). THE GOAL, stated once: a road that genuinely CROSSES a river is carried on a deck from dry road on one side to dry road on the other, continuous with the road network and reading as that road (same asphalt, lane paint, square flush ends), and nothing else gets a deck — Evan, from the river-mouth screenshots (decks along the shore, V meets mid-river, wedges, stubs between decks): "change the rules to avoid situations like this". Roads and rivers are both analytic, so decks are computed GEOMETRICALLY (v1 marched the terrain from each crossing with attach/continue heuristics — slow, and decks still ended in water at path seams; an angle-based span before that ended one crossing's deck inside the next crossing's water): (0) WINDOWS: a chunk looks at ±`BRIDGE_WINDOWS[0]` (900u, warped) around its center; every interaction a chain has with what lies outside it (a wet stretch merging on, an end meeting another, a tee) is within `BRIDGE_EDGE_GUARD` (48u), so a chain with a sample that close to the window's edge is UNDECIDED there — a query whose decks depend on one (a host, a child, an overlapping deck) retries with 1800 and then 3200, where an undecided chain drops (`window`). Chains longer than `BRIDGE_MAX_LENGTH` (1000u) drop whatever the window, which keeps every owned chain ≥ the guard inside the first window (midpoint ≤ 181 + L/2 + the warp's 150u spread + 48 ≤ 900) and a T closure two chains deep inside the last. A single ±800 window was MEASURED to split T-junctions across chunks; one ±2200 window fixed that at ~100× the per-chunk cost. Roads are CLIPPED to the window, not required whole, and the belt's loops, chains and seams are all canonical (smaller end first, seam at the node the river field says is driest, ties by position). (1) `RoadPath`s — only the road within `BRIDGE_ROAD_MARGIN` (60u) of a footprint, inside the window: the network's RUNS, the BELT (city walls with the city on exactly one side, each from the biome cell holding its midpoint, network-free via `getZoneWalls`, chained by endpoint; a closed loop is opened at its node farthest from the rivers), and in-city ARTERIALS (`collectArterialPaths`). Samples sit on CANONICAL lattices — each wall from its own lexicographically smaller end, the arterials on a global 8u coordinate lattice — so every chunk samples a road identically. (2) WET = the terrain's own river field at ANY point of the road's CROSS-SECTION (the centerline or either deck edge, ±W/2), against the distance the terrain yields that road at: the whole footprint (`halfWidth + bank`) for the belt and the arterials — the quay yields there — but only `runRiverYield()` (the water band + 6) for an inter-city RUN, which stays a road on a river's outer bank or beside a pond (step 5 uses the same numbers, so a deck always covers every vertex whose road the terrain gave up; a centerline-only test left a wedge of the road's curbed end showing past an oblique deck's end). A maximal wet stretch (gaps < `BRIDGE_WET_MERGE` 16u merge) is one `WetItem`, extended by the abutment onto the dry road beside it (LANDED ends) — or OPEN where the road itself ends while wet. (3) JOINS: open ends within `BRIDGE_END_MEET` (10u) form a node; its straightest pair continues through as one chain ONLY if nearly straight (directions into the two items ≥ 145° apart, `BRIDGE_JOIN_STRAIGHT_COS`) — two roads meeting at an angle joined into one V-kinked deck; every other end tees onto a road PASSING the node (its item continuing ≥ W/2 either way — "T"s onto another road's end overlapped as wedges) within `BRIDGE_JUNCTION_REACH` (6u), or its chain drops. (4) THE RULES (`deckBuilder`), each dropping a chain with its reason in `bridgeDebug.drops`: a chain between two decks (both ends T) drops — a stub hanging over the water; a belt/arterial chain landed at both ends must CROSS a river centerline an odd number of times (`centerlineCrossings`: built pieces, each extended by its half-width so a pond's round end counts) — a belt rounding a city lobe's tip at a river mouth made both V decks of the screenshot — at no less than `BRIDGE_MIN_CROSSING` (40°); no belt/arterial chain runs along a river inside its footprint (samples within `BRIDGE_ALONG_ALIGN` 35° — the road layer's angle — of the nearest piece) for more than `BRIDGE_MAX_ALONG_SHORE` (60u); RUNS are exempt from the crossing and shore rules (they are the only road between two cities, and a run only yields near the water anyway); no deck turns more than `BRIDGE_MAX_TURN` (45°) within `BRIDGE_TURN_WINDOW` (40u) AFTER its corners are rounded (`filletCorners`: every corner of the path — found on a 5u Douglas–Peucker view, a fine one splits it over short legs — becomes a cubic Hermite tangent to the road on both sides, reaching at most 45% of the way to the next corner and stopping `BRIDGE_FILLET_END_KEEP` (12u) short of an end, radius ≤ `BRIDGE_FILLET_RADIUS` 120 — the corner lies in the river's footprint, where the terrain shows no road, so only the deck bends; a belt crossing at a wall junction reads as a curved viaduct instead of a kink); a T-child meets its host at ≥ `BRIDGE_MIN_T_ANGLE` (35°, two converging roads overlapped at a narrow angle) and on it (≤ `BRIDGE_TEE_OFF_HOST` 2u from the host's rounded centerline). A road that fails them simply has no deck: in a city it ends at the quay, which runs along the bank — the V decks, along-shore viaducts and their T-children of the screenshots are gone; MEASURED over the 13 crossing areas + the reported spots (1095 chunks): 82 → 31 decks, and over 2435 chunks around six city/river areas 90 → 26 (most of the loss is T-children and belts along a shore; every remaining run crossing keeps its deck unless its host belt drops). (4b) CROSSINGS OF THEIR OWN (`bridges/crossings.ts` + `mouths.ts`, Evan on the rules: "everything looks WAY better… but we are missing bridges that we probably should have"; a city river ran 1.4 km between two quays with no deck at all): a river with city on BOTH banks (a piece whose quay offset is city on both sides, `cityRiverOf` per river edge) also gets STRAIGHT decks of its own, at most `CROSSING_MAX_SKEW` (35°) off square to the river, landed on PAVEMENT on both banks — the quay road counts (`landCrossing`: march out until the deck's whole cross-section is dry by the terrain's own river field, then on to the middle of the road met there; both end corners within `CROSSING_CORNER_SLACK` of the curb) — so they can never follow a shore, kink, V or end in the water. Two kinds: ARTERIAL crossings, wherever a city arterial/segment arterial crosses the centerline at ≥ `CROSSING_MIN_ROAD_ANGLE` (20°) with no deck within `CROSSING_SPACING` (120u) — along the arterial, turned toward square until within the skew, arterial-wide, or a street-wide deck when a freeway-wide end would hang off a quay at a slant; and FILL crossings — per maximal in-city stretch of each river edge, round(length / `FILL_STEP` 350u) ≥ 1 slots spread evenly, each suppressed within `FILL_CLEAR` (180u) of any other deck or viable arterial crossing, snapped (≤ `FILL_SNAP_MAX` 80u along the river) onto its district's street line along the grid axis nearest square to the river, preferring one whose street goes on past both quays, a STREET-width deck (`laneScale`, no lane paint). Two crossings in each other's way: the higher `prio` wins (arterial > fill, squarer first), a one-round rule on VIABILITY alone, so every window that sees both decides the same; a crossing's geometry is a pure function of it and cached across windows (`crossingGeoms`); its chain counts as at the window's edge within `CROSSING_EDGE` of it. They are ordinary landed chains to the deck builder (heights, arch, piers, terrain cut, gaps). MEASURED over 75.6 km of in-city river in a 44 km square: crossings 91 → 205, stretches with a gap > 600u 33 → 8, city/river/lake mouths with a crossing 12 of 28 → 23; the 1095-chunk bench 31 → 44 decks; no new overlaps, twins or wet ends (a checker over 375 decks). (4c) FREEWAY MOUTHS (Evan: "some freeways abruptly end at the edge of the city … it would make the most sense to use freeway bridges", red lines: one straight on, one CURVED between two offset arterial mouths; "i like the smaller, random bridges as well so keep them"): a MOUTH is where a city arterial heading into a city river's CHANNEL is last dry across a deck's whole width, past the abutment (`mouthsOf`, per river edge over its whole city stretch like the fill slots, so window-free; a road grazing a bank makes none). Mouths on opposite banks pair greedily by score (chord ≤ `MOUTH_PAIR_MAX` 300u — the deck's midpoint then lies within the crossings' reach —, each arterial ≤ `MOUTH_PAIR_TURN` 50° off the chord, the chord ≤ `MOUTH_PAIR_SKEW` 45° off square) into a FREEWAY-WIDTH deck along a cubic Hermite tangent to both arterials, which must keep every deck's rules (one centerline crossing at ≥ `BRIDGE_MIN_CROSSING`, ≤ `BRIDGE_MAX_TURN`, ≤ `BRIDGE_MAX_ALONG_SHORE`); a mouth without a pair, its arterial ≤ `MOUTH_SINGLE_SKEW` 50° off square, gets a straight freeway deck turned to `CROSSING_MAX_SKEW` (`landCrossing`, nudged ≤ 16u along the river when a quay corner misses the pavement) that must land within `MOUTH_SINGLE_LAND` 24u of the mouth. Verdicts, one round each: a mouth deck overlapping a NATURAL deck gives way (that deck serves it — `mouthPre`); a lone mouth's deck gives way to its pair's; of two mouth decks overlapping, a pair's and then the shorter wins; arterial and fill crossings give way to a mouth deck that passes `mouthPre`. Crossing keys carry the river edge (a mouth at a confluence belongs to two edges — sharing one key let whichever window computed first set the geometry, MEASURED as a deck that changed with query order). MEASURED over a 28 km square (113 arterial mouths): freeway decks at a mouth 55 → 82, dead ends 60 → 31 — the rest run along the bank into the river (no crossing to make) or overlap a natural deck — and of those, lane paint up to the river 53 → 4 (see LANE ENDS); the 1095-chunk bench 44 → 48 decks (street 12 → 9, freeway 32 → 39). (4d) EVERY FREEWAY MOUTH, Y MERGES (Evan: "maybe we need to see what it looks like if ALL freeways separated by river are connected with bridges. This will likely require bridges to be able to merge"; screenshots of two cities on opposite banks with no deck): a mouth is now any freeway's — arterial, segment arterial, BELT, RUN, in a city or between two (`edgeRoadPaths`, every run leg near the edge from each piece's own network) — whose wet stretch touches the edge's footprint. PAIRS are chosen best-first among candidates whose curve keeps the rules (judged in the pairing), `MOUTH_PAIR_MAX` 700 (300 left every wide diagonal crossing unconnected), chord ≤ 50° off square. A mouth left over that faces a paired mouth BRANCHES into that pair's deck (a Hermite arriving at 40/55/70° to the trunk toward the shared mouth, past its ramp, on its side of the centerline where it can so the branch itself crosses, clear of the trunk away from the junction — a T-child emitted with its trunk; a Y); a mouth may also tee into the road's OWN deck landing at the mouth it faces (`resolveNatural`, resolved in the window). Mouths within `MOUTH_CLUSTER` (45u) on one bank are one junction. A LONE mouth gets a STREET-width deck, never a freeway one ("if the bridge is freeway sized, it should connect to freeway on both sides"); an arterial crossing is freeway-wide only with freeway at both ends (`freewayDistanceAt`). Every mouth deck crosses its edge's BUILT centerline exactly once (`builtCrossingRule`: a pond's end or a gap is no crossing), no other river twice, at ≥ 40°, over one wet stretch, ≤ 45u dry from a landed end. Mouth crossings are emitted wherever their geometry meets the window and are at its edge by their whole extent. MEASURED (28 km square): spots of two cities' mouths facing each other across a river with every mouth bridged 13 → 23 of 34, freeway mouths bridged 194 → 250 of 409, road×river crossings decked 70 → 77 of 82; bench 48 → 59 decks. (5) DECKS ARE BUILT LAZILY: a chunk builds the chains whose midpoint lies in it, the hosts their T-ends take a height from, and every deck meeting them (`finishDeck`); the terrain builds every deck reaching into a cell (`getFreewayBridgesNear`, see below). Chains are oriented canonically, so every query agrees on a deck. GEOMETRY: the path is the samples unwarped, Douglas–Peucker-simplified at `BRIDGE_MAX_DEVIATION` (1.5u — 5u left decks askew over the road at their ends; tight, the ends are square to the road they land on and the edges stay on its curbs), corners filleted, then no leg shorter than `BRIDGE_MIN_LEG` (3u). `bridgeSections` lofts cross-sections at `bridgeStations` (trimmed ends, path vertices, parapet-gap and paint boundaries, ≤ `BRIDGE_SEGMENT_LENGTH` 6u apart) with the lateral axis INTERPOLATED between the path vertices' miters — a miter-to-normal switch between two close sections swung an edge backwards and BOW-TIED the slab (wedge-shaped holes, the sand showing through; `bridgeSpec.test.ts` asserts both edges advance between every two sections). A T-child is TRIMMED where its centerline leaves the host's slab (found along the child — W/2/sin θ assumed a straight host and left a crack against a curving one), then moved in until its whole straight cut (center and both corners, along the host's direction there, at the host's grade: `BridgeTrimAxis`) lies `BRIDGE_TEE_OVERLAP` (0.6u) inside the host's slab; the cut is ONE section to the first square section just past its farthest corner (`cutClearance`, against the real axes, `BRIDGE_SWEEP_CLEAR` 0.5u to spare — a square station inside the sweep crossed the cut: dark overlapping triangles at junctions). HEIGHTS: a landed end sits on the (padded) road height; a T end on the host's height at its cut; linear between, a PLAIN deck may be STYLED as an arch (seeded, `archChance`, ≤ min(`maxCamber` 5u, 6% of its length)); every deck landed at both ends is raised by that arch to clear the water it crosses by `BRIDGE_WATER_CLEARANCE` (4.5u), up to max(`BRIDGE_MAX_CAMBER` 16u, 10% of its length), else dropped; a deck with a T end lifts its landed end by at most `BRIDGE_MAX_END_LIFT` (0.5u, the autostep) or carries the clearance arch over its DRAWN range (`bridgeDeckY` — 0 where it meets its host), beside a T end the slab must stand `BRIDGE_JUNCTION_MIN_CLEARANCE` (2u) over the water; clearance is judged along the DECK (its corners are rounded off the road) over the channel only. LANDED ENDS ON PAVEMENT ARE CUT ALONG THE ROAD'S EDGE (`landedCut`, Evan: an oblique deck's parapet walled off the road's far lanes and its side stood as a ledge over them): the end section runs between the two points where the deck's edges leave the pavement for the LAST time within 35% of the deck (cut at the FIRST exit, a slanted landing kept its edge and parapet over the road's side; pavement = `pavedAt`, asphalt + curb and a city's sidewalk, the one definition the cut, the ramp length and the parapet gaps share — the gaps scanned over the WHOLE deck; a road's own chain that then fails clearance or sinks is rebuilt with the first-exit cut, then uncut, `withCutRetry` — never a synthesized crossing, whose retry rode 54u over its road), at the road's heights there (0.05 under), through the T-end machinery (`trimStartAxis`/`trimEndAxis` with a zero `BridgeLanding`), so the road continues onto the deck from any lane; the terrain cut follows the drawn slab (see THE GROUND UNDER A DECK) and respects the oblique cut (`bridgeWithinEnds`); pier columns reach the DRAWN underside. A road PAD (the deck lying on the road station by station) was built and REJECTED from screenshots: chopped asphalt where slab and road fought, piers on land. Other LANDED ENDS RAMP INTO THE ROAD (`bridgeRampAt`, Evan: "the end of the bridge should have a ramp that clips into the floor so there's no ledge"): the slab's end stood as a ledge over the asphalt wherever the road fell away across the deck (MEASURED up to 1.8u — the city plateau beside the belt grade) or ahead of it. Each landed end stores a `BridgeLanding` — a straight cross-fall fitted UNDER the ground there (as the cut draws it) and `BRIDGE_RAMP_DIVE` (0.15u) lower — and over the last `BRIDGE_RAMP_LENGTH` (20u since ≤ 30% of the deck) the deck top blends into it quadratically (tangent to the deck's own line at its start), the parapets rising out of the ground with it (`BridgeSection.wall`); the ribbon and the colliders (slab in `BRIDGE_RAMP_STRIPS` lateral strips at the tilted top's height, walls at their tapered height) read the same sections, so client and server walk the drawn ramp. Every landed end's deck LINE stands `BRIDGE_DECK_LIFT` (1u) over its road (Evan: "raise the bridges a little further … ensure the grass texture is under bridge and the bridge still doesnt have a lip"), the ramp taking the slab down under the road at the end. The terrain cut follows the DRAWN top — ramp and cross-fall included, from `BRIDGE_RAMP_END_KEEP` (3u) in from a landed end, where the slab lies under the road — and off the city the road stays painted under a landed end's whole ramp (the paint fades out over `DECK_PAINT_END_FADE` past it): cut only to the unramped line, the ground stood over the dive and grass showed through the deck (screenshot; MEASURED over the bench's decks: ground above the drawn top 2428 → 844 points, of them showing ground that is not road 31 → 8). PARAPET GAPS (`finishDeck`): each wall is clipped EXACTLY against the strip of every deck that meets it (a T-child's full strip to its host's centerline, an overlapping deck's), shrunk by a wall's width so two walls meeting at a corner overlap rather than leave a notch — a T's opening matches the child's edges at any angle, overlapping decks lose their inner walls and read as one wider deck; a child is not clipped against its host (its cut already ends its walls at the host's edge). CROTCH FILLETS (the child's edge met the host's in a hard point): per T end and side `crotchFlares` fits a fillet (radius 6 acute, 4 obtuse) tangent to both edges; the child's sections WIDEN per side (`BridgeSection.wl`/`wr`, measured against the host line and the circle, `crotchWidening`), flare stations are added (allowed inside the cut's sweep when their quad is convex), the host's wall opens over the fillet (`crotchRings`), and the colliders follow (base strips over the shared width + 1u flare strips each as long as it lies in the slab). `gaps` replaced the old `joins` (symmetric openings ± (W/2)/sin θ + 1 — gaps and stubs at corners). LANE PAINT (`BridgeLanePaint`): the dash phase continues the terrain road's at each landed end (its `freewayAlong` there and its rate going outward, blended between the ends), none within `BRIDGE_PAINT_JUNCTION_CLEAR` (8u) of another deck's edge, and none over `BRIDGE_PAINT_END_CLEAR` (12u) at a landed end whose road has none. THE DECK LOOKS LIKE THE ROAD (`deckMaterial.ts`, Evan: "blending of bridge segments and blending into roads needs to be better/more seamless"): an unlit ShaderMaterial with the city's `road.jpg` at the terrain's world tiling (the ribbon's uvs are world x/z ÷ 26.25 from a WORLD_WRAP multiple, so the tile phase is the terrain's), city_frag's gutter darkening and dashed lane lines at ±freewayWidth/2, the terrain's fake directional shade (the deck top's SHADING normal is straight up, so every piece of a junction — pitched a little differently — shades identically: a lit per-face normal drew a line at every seam), the night dim and the lamp glow; walls and underside keep their vertex colors under the same shade; world curvature via `prepareDressingMaterial`. It is RENDERED AS ONE RIBBON per chunk (`bridgeRibbon`, relative to the chunk's float64 origin, `userData.ownsGeometry`); piers are one instanced mesh through `finalizeInstancedChunk`. COLLIDERS (`bridgeColliderPoints`, client and server): one unrotated fixed body per chord between consecutive sections carrying a TRIANGLE MESH of exactly what the ribbon draws there (`DressingColliderBody.mesh`: the slab's top split along the ribbon's own diagonal, its underside and edges, each STANDING wall as a closed prism capped where it starts or stops; the client's `<TrimeshCollider>`, the server's `ColliderDesc.trimesh`) — pitched boxes per chord (lateral strips, per-part yaw) could not follow a slab that cross-falls and twists between two sections, 8% of the drawn top more than 0.1u off its box and up to 6.7u, and players fell through (Evan); `bridgeSpec.test.ts` asserts the mesh top IS the drawn top and the walls stand exactly where drawn. Piers (`bridgePierColumns`) rise to the drawn slab's underside, the lowest over the column's footprint (from the centerline line + ramp, 9 columns stood up through a cross-falling slab). THE GROUND UNDER A DECK (`computeVertexData` step 7): cut to `BRIDGE_CUT_BELOW_TOP` (0.08u) below the deck's top across its drawn length and half-width — EXACT FOR THE MESH'S TRIANGLES (capping each vertex let a triangle between them rise through a non-planar slab): the slab is modeled as the ribbon's own triangles (`drawnOf`), and `bridgeTriangleCap` lowers each lattice vertex by the worst rise any of its six terrain triangles (every vertex on its reference) makes over the slab inside it, clipped Sutherland–Hodgman against each slab triangle, so nothing is over-cut where the slab is planar; between lattice points the caps are interpolated as the mesh draws them; refs and triangle rises are cached per deck and lattice (uncached, half an LOD1 chunk's build); the overlap query finishes its decks so the cut sees the ribbon's sections; the water is hidden at a vertex under a slab too low to clear it — feathered over `BRIDGE_CUT_FEATHER` (4u) beside it, never below `waterHeight + 0.3` where water is drawn (a lake's too: a deck at a river mouth cut its bank under the lake's level, which showed through beside it) — under the decks that EXIST and nowhere else: the old local cut (every road in a river's footprint, down to the water, with the pit drawn as water) left dark jagged pits, asphalt under the water and water in them wherever a road had no deck (screenshots). The vertex fetches its 256u cell's decks FIRST (`decksAround` → `getFreewayBridgesNear`, cached per cell; enumerating evaluates terrain and clobbers the scratch), skipped while `enumeratingBridges` (the decks' landed ends lie on roads, never under a deck) and on the raw/far paths (pad candidates, far LODs, the main thread's pre-filter). Off the city the road's paint gives way to the ground under and beside the deck too, faded in over `DECK_PAINT_END_FADE` (8u) past each landed end's ramp (a run's curb strip showed in stair steps past a deck's edges), and `VertexResult.underDeck` keeps foliage and every placement out (blades grew through the deck; a lamp stood on one — 2 in the bench areas' 3476 lamps, now 0). The cell lists are padded by `DECK_END_REACH` (40u) for the lane ends below. Piers stand on the raw ground less that cut. Owned by the chunk holding the deck's midpoint (deduped by midpoint), enumerated from the chunk center — keep `DRESSING_CHUNK_SIZE` consistent between client and server. ## Deck seams, depth and lanes

**Deck seams, depth and lanes .** THE MOUTH'S PAINT (`computeVertexData` step 7e, `bridgeMouthFieldAt` in `bridges/deckMouth.ts`): between a landed CUT end and its road's asphalt the ground paints ASPHALT flush against the deck — the curb and sidewalk the road ran across the mouth, and any sand sliver the straight cut leaves against a curving pavement edge — and nowhere else.
