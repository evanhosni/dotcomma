## Rivers

**Rivers — the edges of their OWN voronoi grid, and cities adapt to them** (`water/rivers/network.ts` — its header is the design in one screen — the road layer `roadLayer.ts` and the per-vertex `field.ts`). Rivers are the voronoi edges of a THIRD grid — `RIVER_GRID_SIZE` 2800, jittered sites shifted by `RIVER_GRID_SHIFT` (1237, 811) so it never lines up with the region grid — INDEPENDENT of biomes and roads: a pure function of position, so every chunk, window, worker and the server agree by construction. Three layers:
- **The network** (`getRiverSegments`, cached per river cell, `RiverSegment` pieces in warped space). Sites ±`RIVER_GRID_RADIUS_CELLS` (6) around the query's cell; edges reaching the cell ± `RIVER_TRUST_MARGIN` (2400) are emitted. Only TRUSTED triangles (circumcircle a cell inside the sites) make edges, and junctions are computed from their three sites in a canonical order: an edge is cached by its two sites, and one first built from a distorted border triangle kept wrong junctions for every later query — MEASURED as a chunk's decks changing with what had been queried before. Each edge rolls whether it carries a river against the `<Rivers chance>` of the biome under its midpoint (its own mount, else its region's; 0 for a region that mounts none: "`<Rivers density>` IS the keep chance" below). The desert (0.052) keeps ~5% of its edges, snow/ocean nearly all; a domain whose regions mount no `<Rivers/>` has none at all (home). There is deliberately NO domain-level fallback: a region cannot get rivers it never asked for (LOST_FEATURES.md §5). Edges are cut into `RIVER_SEGMENT_LENGTH` (50u) pieces, and a piece is NOT built (1) `RIVER_MOUTH_REACH` (160u) deep into WATER cells in both directions along its edge — symmetric, so the mouth runs on into the basin and never depends on the walking direction; (2) [REMOVED] a `prohibitRivers` biome the pond-sized footprint would touch — the flag was never set by any biome, so the nine probes ran in the hot loop for nothing (LOST_FEATURES.md §1); (3) on HIGH or STEEP ground (Evan: uphill water) — biome relief > `RIVER_MAX_RELIEF` (40u, the mountain's rock), a grade over the piece and its neighbors > `RIVER_MAX_GRADE` (0.2; Evan raised it from 0.12), a RIDGE (centerline `RIVER_MAX_RIDGE` 6u above both footprint edges) or a HILLSIDE (the footprint's edges differing by > `RIVER_MAX_CROSS_GRADE` 0.15) — judged on a cheap terrain proxy (zone base + biome relief faded by the distance to the cell's edge; a real evaluation would need every biome cell's freeway network over a ~20km window). Only the RELIEF rule is final (`RIVER_BLOCK_MOUNTAIN`): a gap of grade/ridge/hillside pieces up to `RIVER_GAP_FILL` (800u) with river or water on both sides — a built piece, a water piece, or the edge's junction in a water zone — IS built (Evan: "ensure the rivers actually connect to their nearby bodies of water", screenshot: one river in three blobs between two seas; the desert's and the snow's own region bases exceed the grade and hillside limits, so the rules cut ordinary rivers everywhere — MEASURED over a 40 km square: gaps under 800u between two stretches 159 → 54, stretch ends under 800u short of the water along their edge 40 → 15, every one left touching the mountain's rock or a road-suppressed stretch; built pieces +24%); (4) in a stretch under `RIVER_MIN_STRETCH` (300u) between two unbuilt pieces (MEASURED: otherwise 197 of 363 stretches were pond blobs under 150u) — after the gap fill, and never one joining water to water. (5) GORGES (Evan, screenshots 86/87/88: two ponds facing each other across a low ridge). Two gap kinds the fill above never joins: a JUNCTION GAP (`junctionGorgesAt` — the fill is per edge and treats the junction as high ground: a run from a stretch into a junction where another river goes on, or where another edge's run comes out of its own stretch, up to `RIVER_GAP_FILL` in all) and a gap INSIDE an edge across TALL RELIEF that is not rock. The relief rule's `RIVER_BLOCK_MOUNTAIN` is only the mountain's ROCK where its biome is DOMED (`RiverEdge.rock`): the dunes (50–200u of relief) and the grass hills (up to 100u) read as mountain everywhere, and image 88 was a desert river in two ponds across a 23u rise over dunes. A gap of high pieces and non-rock relief is built when the REAL ground (`gorgeGroundAt`) rises less than the gorge's carve limit (then `RIVER_GORGE_MAX_RISE` 25u; now `<Rivers maxCarve>`, 80) above the straight line between the two water surfaces it joins. The proxy misread those rises by up to 35u (it ignores the zone blend: dunes beside another region's base) — 88 read 25.9 against a real 22.8 — so the rise is measured on the real terrain, with the wall pass and the shore state SAVED AND RESTORED around it (`saveWallPass`/`saveShore`): the network is built mid-vertex in places (a city's waterfront asks for the pieces near a wall). MEASURED over an 80 km square: 142 gorges (127 junction gaps over high ground, 5 junction and 10 in-edge gaps across non-rock relief); left: 15 high junction gaps and 19 tall-relief gaps, every one rising ≥ 22u by the end-surface measure (the snow ridges like 87: 30–68u), and the rock as before. Decided from the edges ON THEIR OWN (`riverEdgeOwnBlocked`) through the junction's river-cell window (`riverJunctionEdges` — never a cell entry, which would cascade), so a pure function of the junction; the road layer still wins over a gorge. The gorge's SURFACE (`gorgeSurfaces`, surface.ts) runs straight from the water where the stretch stops to the junction's (the continuing river's surface there, else the lowest-ridge pair's line — one value per junction), under any road cap, then a running minimum from its higher end — never uphill (a cap easing back up ran it uphill at one gorge in 117); the channel carve then cuts the ridge at the river's normal width and depth, banks blending up over `bank`, and the bed paint stops at the steep walls by itself (`capRiverBed`). A gorge may DROP steeply: up to 18u in one piece (grade 0.37), in the desert. Every natural end of a built stretch — where a piece is not built, or at a junction no other BUILT river leaves — rolls once for a `<Pond riverEnd>` kind; a miss ends it ROUND at its own width ("Every river end rolls" below: the old always-a-pond ends and the fizzle are gone). WIDTH is per junction — ±25% jitter × its `<Rivers width>` × (1 + the widening toward a region of lakes, `<Lake riverWidening riverWideningReach>`), capped at `RIVER_WIDTH_MAX` — interpolated along each edge, so rivers widen toward the ocean. NOTHING IS CUT FOR A CITY: a version that cut rivers out of the city and dropped the stubs was REJECTED from a screenshot (blobs beside every city, a river ending 80u short of the sea); the city adapts instead. Two trace-based designs on the biome walls were REJECTED before that (window- and order-dependent: rivers ended at cell borders, sat inside city lobes, missed the sea).
- **The road layer** (`riverPieceSuppressed`; Evan: the road wins). Where a run, the belt or an in-city arterial runs within 35° of a river (`RIVER_ROAD_ALIGN_COS`) inside its footprint (+ the road's half-width) for `RIVER_ROAD_ALONG_MIN` (500u) or more — longer than a deck should be — that stretch is not built and the river ends BLUNTLY on either side (its last built piece keeps full width — a round capsule end), so the road stays dry. Evan prefers blunt ends: the 150u fizzle toward a suppressed stretch (`RIVER_ROAD_TAPER`, `riverRoadScale`) was removed; the network's own dead ends keep their seeded pond/fizzle. Crossings keep their decks: the angle gate is what keeps a GRID of separate crossings (the city's arterials) from reading as one long stretch (an angle-free "road inside the footprint" rule wiped the rivers out of whole cities). Lazy per piece — it needs the freeway network of the piece's biome cell — cached on the edge; skipped for edges with no city within `FREEWAY_LINK_CELLS` (no road can reach them).
- **The road layer's second half: no freeway severed** (`riverEdgeRoadLayer`; `riverPieceSuppressed(e, i)` reads it). Per edge, from the roads near its pieces (`edgeRoadPathsUnbuilt` — built from the edge's own raw pieces, so the layer never recurses into the river field), every WET stretch of a road is judged by the bridges' own rules (odd crossings of this edge, ≥ 40°, ≤ 60u along the shore, ≤ 45° of turn after rounding — `deckPathTurn`); a stretch no deck may carry (SHALLOW, a belt/run ALONG the shore, a belt/run ending in the water with nothing to tee into) gives the river way, in passes until none is left. KEPT: a GRAZE (the road goes on round it or a deck lands beside it — removing grazes took out whole river mouths and 14 of 59 bench decks), anything within 20u of a deckable stretch, a belt the waterfront carries (within 40° of the river), and a city arterial the QUAY carries (everything but a shallow crossing). Then NO RIVER END UNDER A ROAD: an end — natural, at unbuilt land, at a gap the road won (INCLUDING one that runs on to the edge's end), or at a junction every other river gave up (≤ `RIVER_JUNCTION_RETRACT` pieces) — retracts piece by piece while a run's/belt's centerline is inside the end piece's footprint ( pond; the round-15 run deck grazing an end; a pond pinching a belt corner to a sliver). Pure per edge (+ the junction's other edges' layers BEFORE retraction). The census `getSeveredFreeways(box)` (`bridges/severed.ts`) lists every connection still cut; `severedFreeways.test.ts` pins the example spots at 0.
- **The per-vertex field** (`riverFieldAt`, run FIRST in `computeVertexData`: building a biome cell's river list evaluates terrain and clobbers the wall-pass scratch). Per biome cell, the pieces that survive the road layer within the widest possible reach, with the water SURFACE at both ends of each (`riverSurfaceAt`: the terrain at that centerline point — its own wall pass, the city at its plateau FLOOR (a surface hung from the freeway grade had blocks under it), lifted with a lake's shore — minus `WATER_BELOW_GROUND` 2.5, deeper than the lake's 1.5 so the quay road stays above the water — and near a lake EASED ONTO THE LAKE'S LEVEL over its `<Lake riverApproach>` (250u) from its wall, never below it on land and never above it in the water: hung from the terrain alone, a mouth's surface stood ~11u over the lake at the wall and dropped to it over one 50u piece, a stepped, jagged sheet of water (MEASURED at famous-shallows; screenshot). In the water the channel stays sunk under the lake, or the bank rule would raise a levee across the lakebed). A piece end's surface also stands `RIVER_ROAD_CLEARANCE` (5u) under the lower landing grade of every run and belt crossing the two pieces beside it (`roadCrossingCap` over `edgeCrossingsOf`, a pure function of the edge so neighbors agree): a river running along a ridge crest the road came through in valleys had its banks held up as a levee and needed a 43u arch, so the run got no deck (Evan, famous-meadow). The landings are sampled ALONG THE ROAD (`alongRoad`, following its legs) — straight on along the crossing leg a belt turning at a corner left its road and read a grade 36u under it at (-1551, 1084), sinking the river into a gorge under the deck — and past the two piece ends beside a crossing its cap rises `RIVER_CAP_GRADE` (0.12, 6u per piece) per unit along the river, so a cap never drops the water within one piece. Only the river comes down; roads, cuts and grades are untouched. The surface is interpolated along the piece: a function of the CENTERLINE only, so it is level across the channel (sampling the centerline with each vertex's own zone weights tilted it). The distance is measured from a MEANDERED query point (`RIVER_MEANDER_AMP/SCALE`, a straight edge winds) and NORMALIZED by the local width factor — `distanceToRiverCenter` is in FACTOR-1 units, so `river.halfWidth/depth/bank` (40/9/36) describe a factor-1 river and every consumer (channel, bank, bed paint, bridges, `riverKeepOff` filters) compares against them as is. Pieces of ONE river combine by a plain min (their joints never notch); DIFFERENT rivers by a compact smooth minimum (`RIVER_FILLET` 24 factor-1 units: weights h² with h = 1 − gap/FILLET), the width factor and surface weighted the same way, so confluences get a fillet and nothing jumps where the nearest river switches. Within one river the width factor, the surface and the bed limit's station are read off each RUN's (an edge's consecutive built pieces) PLAINLY nearest piece — the projection onto collinear pieces moves continuously — never the nearest by factor-1 distance, which jumps between pieces wherever the width varies (a pond's start, a widening toward the sea): MEASURED 10.9u of surface at (-6300, 3511), the image-84 cliff the same; a river scan over ±8000 went from 203 of 240 feature points with a height jump > 0.1u to 0. The quay reads a second combination from the STRAIGHT (un-meandered) point — distance, width factor and direction all from it, by the same rule.

BESIDE QUAYS (`holdRiverBesideQuays`, water/joins/; keyed on `<Quays/>` since the final cleanup, on crispness before): the city's crisp wall pass gives the lake no weight, so its side draws no lake and no mouth — a surface interpolated toward a piece end in the lake (sunk there under the lakebed) showed as river water 14u under the lake beside it at (3064, 1310). Step 4 holds the river surface up to the lake's level by the crisp land's weight (on land fading with the shore lift, on the lake side the city's own weight, 1 at the wall from both sides), and the decks clear that drawn surface (`drawnRiverSurface`); nothing changes where no crisp zone is near a lake. THE CHANNEL (step 4) is FORCED, not min'ed: a parabola from `surface − depth·√factor` at the centerline to a RIM at `surface + SHORE_RISE` at the half-width (the water's edge sits just inside), the bank blending the rim into the terrain over `bank` — raising low ground as well as cutting high ground (a min() alone left low far banks under the water). Water is drawn out to `halfWidth + bank/2` (the water band, past the waterline so the shore triangles are level), and ground LOWER than the rim is HELD at the rim across that whole band before it descends (`smoothstep(waterBand, reach)` instead of from the half-width): blending it down from the half-width put the bank under the surface, and the water mesh drew a second, stair-stepped strip on the dry bank (screenshots; the ring around a pond end was the same). The water mesh and the terrain share one grid and interpolate linearly on the same triangles, so the vertex-level guarantee — no vertex outside the channel reports water above its ground — holds across every triangle at every LOD (`banks.test.ts`, LOD1/LOD2/LOD3 spacings; 2016 such vertices over four 3km squares before). AT A MOUTH the rim is NOT forced (`riverMouthShare`, `joins/`): where the ground before the carve lies under the lake drawn there, the carve only deepens it, the river surface comes down to the lake level and the bed paint is pushed out of reach; the share ramps from 1 at the level to 0 at `level + SHORE_RISE`, so ground under the water stays under it and dry land is carved as before. Before it the rim stood a dry LEVEE in the sea between river and lake at 138 of 186 mouths over a 45km square (a sand ring around the mouth pond, Evan, screenshot), up to 28u above the lakebed; after, 0, with outputs differing only within 25u of such points (`banks.test.ts` "open into the lake"). A RIVER BESIDE A LAKE MERGES WITH IT (`riverLakeMerge`, `joins/`; Evan, screenshot: a river along an ocean shore, snow beyond, a long levee of bank and shore sand between the two waters — "the river should merge into the ocean, as if they are the same shared joinable biome"). Every piece end also records its centerline's SIGNED distance to the nearest water wall and the lake level there (`riverPointAt`, interpolated like the surface: `riverSample.shore`/`level`; the distance clamped to ±`SHORE_REACH` 410u, where lakeSurface stops measuring — a 2000u cap interpolated against a measured end stood a 10u cliff mid-piece). Where the gap between the river's water band and the wall is under `40 + min(band, 120)/2` (fading over 80u), the land BETWEEN them — by the triangle inequality the vertex's river distance plus its own wall distance exceeds the centerline's by under 0.6–1.5 half-widths (so the far bank never qualifies), OR a sliver within 25u (fading over 50u) of both the river's water band and the wall, the corner where a river turns into the lake — and the lake's beach there sink to the RIVER CHANNEL'S OWN FLOOR (`level − depth·√factor`, min'ed, so the carve and the deeper bowl toward the lake win): a 3u floor left the old levee showing through the water's 2.5u alpha fade as a pale sandbar (Evan). The water there is the level (in the band the river's own water eased onto it by the share, so the two meshes agree) and the ground is painted as the river's whole BED, never as the land. The piece-end surface is eased onto the level by the same closeness 80u wider (`riverPieceEndSurface`; MEASURED 1.6u over the lake 135u off the shore). Off the city and 10–40u past any freeway's half-width AND the city's own wall (even where a drowned belt is pushed off it: sunk beside it, the lake's beach stood 9u under the crisp city edge) — quays, waterfronts, a run along the shore and every deck landing stay as they were — not on ground 10–20u over the shore height (a real ridge: dunes 30–80u), and not where the river stands over the centerline's level by 0.25–0.75u (the lake's own level tilts ~0.6u over 80u, so the vertex's level is the wrong reference). Under the level nothing is gated (a mouth's surface sinks fast: a gate there cut the share within 6u, a 10u cliff). `passesPlacementFilters` rejects any point under drawn water. ## Lakes, shores and the Water system

**Lakes, shores and the Water system** (`water/lakes/` + `computeVertexData` step 4 (`water/index.ts` `resolveWater`) + `world/water/material.ts` + the water child mesh in `TerrainRenderer.tsx`). LAKES are a biome with a `<Lake depth>` (the ocean region's lake biome, `joinable`, so adjacent lake cells are one body): the water sits at a LEVEL = the region base at the cell SITE − 1.5, BLENDED across every water cell within 1.5 cells by a compact kernel (`lakeLevelAt`) — one level per cell was MEASURED as 1–3u walls of water on the wall between two lake cells; the kernel's support fits inside the 5×5 grid window, so it never steps at grid borders. The lake's terrain is defined RELATIVE TO THE LEVEL, not the base: `level + SHORE_RISE` at its wall (presence 0, seen from either side) descending to `level − depth` at full presence. LAND NEAR WATER NEVER SITS BELOW THE WATER: any non-water vertex within its lake's `shoreHold` (160u) of a water zone's wall is lifted onto `level + SHORE_RISE`, fading back to its own relief over its `shoreFade` (250u) (`shoreLift`, also applied inside `blendedTerrainAt` so a river surface or road grade sampled beside a lake is lifted with the ground around it) — the desert dipped 100u under the ocean's level beside it, a wall of water seen from the dunes. On the WATER side the blend may not dip under the bowl's own height (`bowlFloor`, only where a neighbor has weight): the lake's side of its wall used to carry its lower neighbor's share unlifted while the land side was lifted onto the shore — a waterline step of up to 5u (measured at a salt|lake junction). Where a river runs close beside the lake the lifted shore between them goes UNDER the level instead (the Rivers paragraph: a river beside a lake merges with it). `VertexResult.waterHeight` (NaN where there is no water in reach) becomes a second mesh per terrain chunk — a child of the chunk plane on pooled geometry with a `waterDepth` attribute, sharing the chunk's lifecycle (`Chunk.water`, `releaseWater`) — drawn by `getWaterMaterial()`: swell in the vertex shader, two scrolling fbm normal octaves, fresnel, sun glint, depth-tinted color, alpha fading out over the last 2.5u of depth (a hard discard, and then a 0.6u fade, traced the LOD triangles as a staircase along every shore), night dim; dry vertices of the water mesh dive by at least the vertex spacing under the LOWER of their ground and the water level beside them (3u let one wet vertex of a coarse LOD — a river is narrower than its quads — spread a water sheet over the banks until LOD1 arrived; dived under the ground alone, a dry vertex on a high bank stood far above the water and the shore triangle rose toward it: a WALL of water drawn as a fall in front of every steep shore, Evan Oct 2026. `chunkGeometry.ts dryWaterLevel` takes the lowest of the vertex's own surface and its eight wet neighbours' — four left a few walls through diagonal corners. ## Steep rivers, peak pools, springs and the flow (Oct 2026; the rock exception, peak pools, springs and sources are gone since Nov 2026 — history)

**The mountain's rock is built whatever its slope.** Evan asked for waterfalls: "allow the existing rivers to go up very steep inclines (no limit on slope) and then wherever the peak is, create a pool, either on top of the mountain … or in a cave". The relief rule's `RIVER_BLOCK_MOUNTAIN` no longer blocks a DOMED biome's rock (`classifyRiverPieces`: a rock piece is built, flagged `e.rock[i] = 1`, and skips the grade/ridge/hillside tests too); a tall relief that is not rock (dunes, hills) is blocked as before and stays gorgeable. The foot's steep non-rock pieces are HIGH, filled by the existing gap fill between the plains river and the rock. The surface still follows the terrain at every piece end (`riverPointAt`), so a river up a 40° face is a 40° sheet of water — the waterfall — and the channel is carved relative to it as everywhere.

**Peak pools** (`riverSurface.ts riverEdgePools`): along a rock stretch, every local maximum of the terrain surface with ≥ 5u of prominence over the two piece ends either side becomes a pool: its three piece ends share the peak's level, and the pool's ends are 1.6× wider (`RIVER_POOL_FACTOR`, applied to the FIELD's piece widths in `buildRiverCellList`, so the taper over the neighbours is linear). The banks come for free: `carveRiverChannel` FORCES the rim at surface + SHORE_RISE and raises low ground to it, so the downhill sides of a pool are banked up ("terrain will need to adjust to make banks around pool"), and the water drops from the pool's edge to the next piece end's surface over one 50u piece. A pool level at the peak's terrain − 2.5 (not the max of the stretch) keeps the cut and the fill small; a pool spanning more pieces would dig or bank by the slope's whole rise. Overlapping peaks: the higher wins. Only the field's widths widen (the bridges and the bed-limit stations read the piece's own): a pool on a mountain is never decked.

**Springs** (`riverPieceRules.ts placeSprings`, `underground/springs.ts`, `caves.ts generateSpring`): with chance 0.5 per edge a river entering a rock run goes 2 pieces (100u) in and the pieces beyond are `RIVER_BLOCK_SPRING` — never filled or gorged — so the river ENDS in the mountain; entered from both junctions a run ≥ 5 pieces gets a spring from each side, a shorter one crosses. The stretch keeps its width (no pond end) and narrows over its last 120u to `RIVER_SPRING_MOUTH_FACTOR` (0.28: an 11u half-width at factor 1) so it fits a cave mouth. The springs are derived from the FINAL blocked array (`riverEdgeSprings`), so a stretch the road layer or the blob rule removed has none. The cave at the mouth is the underground's (underground/DECISIONS.md "Spring caves"): a flooded passage to a pool room, the river's source — "rivers can also go into a lower cave and not really have a waterfall" is the same cave on a gentle foot.

**The water's flow** (`field.ts`, `waterFlowX/Z` on VertexResult, the `waterFlow` attribute of the water mesh): per vertex, the plainly nearest piece's direction turned downhill (the sign of its two end surfaces), scaled by its grade (0.35 on the flat, 1 from a 6% grade), blended across runs like the surface, zero on a lake and stilled over a mouth by the mouth share. The water shader (`world/water/material.ts`) advects its noise normals along it in two phases cross-faded on a triangle wave (each restarts while the other is whole), adds a 7u ripple running down the flow and pale streaks growing with it; still water keeps its drift, with a third, finer octave and a third swell (Evan: "it currently looks too static"). The advection is a translation of the wrapped position, so the fbm's period survives (a rotated flow frame would seam at every 4200u wrap).

## The rivers rebuilt from their spec (Nov 2026)

The river code (this folder, the river parts of `vertexCompute.ts` steps 2–5 and of `lakes.ts`) was rewritten from the rethink spec (describe → rebuild → audit). Placement is UNCHANGED: the grid, the sites (`${seed} - river - ${ix}X${iz}`), the keep roll (`river edge` by the region-grid cell under the warped midpoint), the junction widths (`river width`, ±25% × the ocean boost) and the pond/fizzle coin (`river end`) were matched to the old code's output, edge for edge (121 of 121 edges in a 24 km square: same junctions, same rolls, same base widths). The sections above that name removed functions (`riverEdgeOwnBlocked`, `riverPieceSuppressed`, `roadCrossingCap`, `riverPointAt`, …) are history.

## The water audit (Nov 2026): every knob a prop

- **Every per-biome/region tunable is a prop**, resolved biome → region → default and blended by zone weight wherever two biomes meet (world/water/README.md "The props"); every default is the old constant, so the world is unchanged but where noted below. Truly global numbers (the grid, the piece length, the tile, the frozen map, the march resolution) stay constants. Where every weighing zone agrees, the value is taken as is, never re-blended: a weighted average of equal values is not bit-equal to them, and the census would have flagged a world-wide float drift.
- **The walls' reach IS the field's support** (`WALL_DEFAULTS.wallReach = RIVER_FIELD_READ`), and a `wallReach` past it is a content error: a wall still easing out where the field stops reading steps there ("Cliffs at the field's edge"). *Superseded by "The valleys and the wide walls": the field reads walls out to the widest reach mounted, and the content error is gone.*
- **River width is per junction, depth per vertex.** A junction's factor already varies linearly along every edge, so `<Rivers width>` there keeps a river continuous across regions and every reader of the pieces (the road layer, the decks, the city) sees the same footprint; `<Rivers depth>` only shapes the carve, so it blends by zone weight at the vertex.
