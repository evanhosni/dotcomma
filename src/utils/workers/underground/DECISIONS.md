# Tunnels: decisions and measurements

The live decisions first, then the record of what was tried and rejected (kept, marked, so nothing is tried twice). Measurements marked "previous implementation" were taken before the Oct 2026 rebuild from spec; the rebuild keeps the same rules and budgets, and its own numbers are the second cost paragraph.

## Live decisions

**An overlay, with ONE height step.** Generation READS the ground; a heightfield cannot overhang, so openings are cut per pixel in the terrain shader (analytic capsules) and the player ignores the heightfield where it is overhead. The one exception is the MOUTH BULGE (mouthBulge.ts), asked for so a mouth stands proud of its slope: `computeVertexData` adds it (step 6, before the pads), from caves generated on the UN-bulged ground (`generatingTunnels`, systems.ts, leaves it out of every height the generator reads), so a mouth never depends on its own bulge and no seed moves. It is zero near roads, freeways, rivers and water: there the pipeline caches road and deck data, and a cache filled during a generation (no bulge) and one filled outside it must agree. Far visual LODs (the raw path) leave it out, like the pads. Cost: per vertex one tile lookup (40k mountain vertices 89 → 91ms warm; the last tile is remembered, `mouthsNear` 24 → 12ns per vertex against ~3.6µs for the whole vertex); a cold 128u tile near the mountains generates the caves in reach once (~0.5s per mountain area, in each worker that asks). A biome declares its caves in its spec (`caves: true`) because the terrain workers and the server never see the `<Caves/>` mount; the rocks, the foliage keep-off and the snow read the same flag from the live config.

**Main-thread cold generation is accepted.** `snowSpeedFactor` (every character step) and the safety nets' height reads can generate a tile's caves on the main thread near a mountain the first time it is touched (tens of ms). No pre-warm.

**Surface nets over a field, not swept tubes (caves only).** Junctions, blind ends, loops and rooms all come out of one isosurface with no special cases. Bricks share their boundary samples and own their lower edges, so they meet without cracks. Pieces carry their system and PATH: pieces of one path union plainly (a smooth union of consecutive segments put a bulge at every joint), paths of one system meet in the smooth junction (2.5u), systems overlap. Per sample a piece only counts inside its bounds padded by `FIELD_REACH` (4.5u; outside, it counts as that far), and the wall noise and floor bumps are drawn only within 3.5u of a wall (`NOISE_SHELL`): both are functions of the point alone, so bricks and chunks still read the same bits from any subset of the pieces. A brick no piece's air can reach is skipped (by the pieces' bounds), and a brick provably all rock by a SOUND lower bound per piece (`boxIsRock`: the brick's distance to the lifted axis less the fullest swing, through the junction's deepest dip; formations only add rock) too — pinned by a test to change no bit. Paths are sampled every 8u (`PATH_STEP`; 4u put ~7 consecutive tubes inside every sample's bounds, and the chunk took 20% longer); formations step every 4u, between the samples.

**Cost (measured, previous implementation).** A field evaluation is 0.125µs in plain Node (esbuild bundle) but ~3.5µs under jest: never judge speed from jest. Per chunk in the worker: cave ~100–210ms (with a mouth ~210, the patch's per-vertex `computeVertexData`; +9–13k triangles), sewer ~15–100ms; cave generation ~19–24ms per cell; the player probe ~5µs and ~5.6KB per frame; `driveUnderground` ~1.2µs per frame average; the snow speed factor ~0.5µs per step warm; a snow sheet ~65ms and ≤ 17k triangles per chunk; a cave chunk ≤ ~90k triangles. A Lipschitz skip of 2u cells inside a brick saved only 11% of the evaluations and was dropped; blocks of 4³ samples settled by the sound bound skipped 73% of samples (154 → 125ms; blocks of 2, 3 or 6 measured the same or worse).

**Cost (measured, the Oct 2026 rebuild, after its optimization pass).** Plain Node (esbuild bundle), the overworld config, the first mountain caves around the origin; "warm" = the terrain's own caches filled. Cave CHUNK with a mouth (64k triangles, 7k patch vertices, 83 column colliders): 658 → 433ms warm (510ms with the drawn-ground lattice cold); over 20 chunks of six systems 251 → 172ms per chunk. Four steps, each pinned bit-identical by a digest of every buffer: (1) the wall and bump noises keep their last point (a sample near a path is inside ~5 consecutive tubes' reach, and each asked simplex3 for the same point: 18% of the chunk) 658 → 567; (2) a brick deeper than FIELD_REACH under its column's lowest drawn ground reads the tunnel field alone (the ground term cannot win there), and the others read the ground once per (x, z) column — meshBrick samples y innermost for it — 567 → 470; (3) inside a brick, blocks of 4³ samples whose box padded by a voxel is provably rock (`boxIsRock`, now sound for three or more paths meeting in one box: a system of P paths dips P − 1 junction depths) are not sampled but filled with a positive stand-in — every cell touching them has rock at all eight corners, so the values are only ever sign-tested — 470 → 452 (40% of the samples of a mouth chunk settled: 1.1M → 650k evaluations over 223 bricks; the rest is the field itself: 10.7M piece bound checks and 3.6M piece evaluations per chunk); (4) the cave mesh and its colliders SHARE vertices (the nets' vertex written on first use, indexed after) 452 → 433, and the chunk's vertices 192k → 32k (three per triangle before: 5.4MB per chunk to transfer and upload). Sewer chunk at a manhole: 133 → 15ms, 151k → 8.7k triangles (over a system 54 → 11.5ms per chunk, 229k → 13k): the ground cut split EVERY triangle within 16u of its column's lowest ground into up to 64 pieces, and a sewer is wholly within 16u of the street; now a triangle whose top lies under the lowest lattice point of the cells its footprint touches (plus the slack) is emitted whole — split, every piece was kept, and the pieces of a flat triangle draw as it does (checked: area, area-weighted centroid, color and daylight, collider area and the liquid agree to 1e-8; every drawn triangle is under the ground + slack at 15 points, both before and after). Cave generation: 3.4ms per cell warm (8 systems in 14 cells, 5.9ms per system), 30.7ms per cell in a cold process (the first cells 103 and 65ms, the terrain's caches filling). The mouth bulge per terrain vertex: `mouthsNear` 13ns (no mouths) to 28ns, with `mouthBulgeAt` ~20ns, against 3.3µs for the whole vertex (a 97 × 97 LOD1 chunk at a mouth: 31.8ms through the full pipeline, 30.7ms raw) — left alone. `terrainSnowDepthFrom` 238 → 113ns per vertex (2.24 → 1.07ms per LOD1 chunk): the amount, the wide amount and the depth each read the drift and lump noises (three simplex2) for the same vertex; they are read once and passed down (the sheet reads them once per node too; its cost is the lattice reads: 20ms per 256u chunk warm, 31 with the lattice cold, 1.6–8k triangles; unchanged). `snowSpeedFactor` 0.28µs per step warm. Rocks 1.9–2.8ms per chunk (50 at a mouth). The player probe: a cell's field compiles in 1.8µs (12 pieces), one evaluation is 0.17µs, the probe with its floor search 1.8µs. The per-frame driver (`driveFades`, the far mouths, `lod1ShareAt`, the mounts' frame loop) allocates nothing: which mouths are LOD1 sites is re-read only when the loaded set changes, the far mouths sort an index array over scratch distances, the doors' hinges and open flags are per-chunk arrays (not measured: reasoned from the code; `pickHoles` still builds its lists, every 10 frames or on a change).

**The road warp's inverse is approximate.** `unwarp` runs 3 fixed-point iterations; at the warp's steepest (gradient ~0.9) it lands tens of units off. Moot for the sewers: the city's blocks are laid out in WORLD space (`getCityTerrain(x, z, …)` gets the raw position), so streets are straight world lines and need no inverse.

**Terrain ignored relative to the tunnel FLOOR, not the feet.** Measured against the feet, a player standing on the slope always had it underfoot and walked up over the mountain. Ignoring ALL terrain while inside left the mouth's floor cap 1u proud of falling ground: an unclimbable lip. Now everything above the ground is cut from the tunnel collider, the heightfield is ignored only where it stands more than an autostep (0.5u) over the tunnel floor (the first rock under the probe point; where there is none, just past a mouth, the feet), and the lip is walked on the terrain. The terrain is excluded only through the step's `ignoreTerrain` (heightfields tagged at creation): never by weakening the fall-through defenses.

**Out of a mouth: the terrain by the tunnel's AIR, and no backstop in front of an opening (Oct 2026).** Evan could walk into the cave at (-3604, -17706) but not out. Three causes, found in order with a server-test replay of the client's step over the real colliders and a per-frame log of the live client: (1) a 1.2u LEDGE inside: the first tube's round end cap kept its end floor flat ~5u back under the mouth, whose floor climbs toward the opening, so the union's floor dropped 1.28u over 1.5u (50°) — walked down, never up. A tube's floor now RISES past its ends into the cap (`TUBE_CAP_FLOOR_RISE` 0.45, steeper than any joined piece), so the neighbour's floor wins across a joint; 134 tube walks in that system, 2 stuck → 0. (2) The FLOOR rule flipped every frame at the opening: the heightfield is linear between 4.4u vertices, so just inside an opening the hill face stands 0.3–0.8u over the floor, and read at the player's column it crossed the 0.5u autostep with every 0.1u of motion — ignored, the capsule moved into the face; solid, it stood inside it and was pushed back (MEASURED in the page: the flag alternating each frame, the floor read steady). A footprint minimum, hysteresis, and cutting the heightfield COLLIDER under the openings were each tried and dropped (the coarse cut triangles became new slopes to perch on; the cut proved unnecessary). The rule is now the tunnel's air: ignored while the probe point is in it, ground again past the opening plane. (3) The wall and floor backstops pushed the player back in from just past the opening plane, where the field closes the mouth with rock — open air over the ground, read as a wall embed every frame. They now skip points in front of an opening (`inFrontOfMouthOpening`). The live walk in and back out passes after (1)–(3); the server replay alone missed (3), since it does not run the backstops.

**Manholes catch.** The shaft's walls start below the street, so a walking player crossed the hole in a few frames. Entering an open manhole's column with the feet within 1u under the street snaps the player to its middle and holds walking until they are 2u under the street. Holding jump climbs at 7u/s; near the street it sets the player down at the exit point. A CLOSED manhole is solid street from 0.5u under it; below that the climb stops 0.15u under the cover and hangs; only clicking it open (from the shaft, or from the junction floor looking up through the shaft) lets the player out.

**Cave floors follow a level baseline.** The highest floor the rock allows climbed at the full grade for hundreds of units under a peak. Floors aim at a line between the mouths' inner floors (with a ±3u swell, faded to nothing at a mouth) and dip only where the rock needs it; no step climbs or falls faster than 0.3 rise/run.

**Mouth shape.** A straight taper put the wide end in the open air (the slope crosses the tube further in), so the opening read as the passage's size. Mouths keep full width for 60% of their section, then narrow. The outline is RAGGED (four sines of the angle about the axis and the distance along it, up to ±24%, dying out at the passage): a plain capsule cut the slope in a clean ellipse.

**High mouths, the hood and the ledge.** Caves only sat at the mountains' feet (asked for higher ones): 60% of entry mouths climb on up their search ray to 20–100u over the foot (180u targets mostly overshot the flanks), with a LEDGE in front levelling the ground 0.08u under the floor over 1.5 radii (fading in from the opening's plane: at the plane it dug into the rim's shoulders; levelled exactly to the floor it z-fought the cave's floor mesh). Asked for mouths that read more vertical: a taller, wider MOUND (0.9 radii over 9 radii) and a HOOD rising over the section from the opening (over 1 radius) to the tube's crown plus 0.3 radii of rock, shouldered off over 5.2 radii beside it and trailing 5 radii past its inner end (shoulders 1.6 → 2.6 → 5.2 and tail 1 → 2.5 → 5 as asked, to blend further into the mountain). A rise of 0.5 radii stood a cliff the LOD1 lattice (4.375u) could not draw.

**Walls cut against the DRAWN ground.** The terrain is DRAWN as 4.375u triangles (LOD1: 420u chunks, 96 segments), while the walls were cut where they rose above the EXACT ground: where the drawn triangles stood higher, the wall stopped below the drawn surface and a strip of missing wall showed under the terrain's cut edge. Everything that meets the ground is judged against the drawn surface itself (`drawnGround.ts`: the LOD1 lattice heights, float32 as uploaded, interpolated over its triangles, each cell split along the (0,1)–(1,0) diagonal like chunkGeometry.ts). Farther LODs (17.5u+) can still differ; at that distance the strip is subpixel-ish.

**Entrances are ONE surface.** A heightfield with a per-pixel hole and a separate wall mesh trimmed at it always met along a line: a crease, a hairline, a color change, a crack at some angle; every cover tried (below) looked wrong somewhere (asked for seamless, "even if it takes major rewriting"). Inside a mouth's footprint the cave mesher meshes min(tunnel field, drawn ground − y), so ground and walls share vertices; ground vertices are snapped onto the drawn ground exactly and take the terrain's normals (the terrain worker's six-face vertex normals interpolated as the GPU does); every vertex gets the terrain's per-vertex fields from `computeVertexData`. Drawn with the terrain's own program (an ENTRANCE variant), the patch is the terrain across the overlap band (1.5u) where the terrain still draws, so their overlap cannot show. A whole-triangle split between terrain and wall materials drew the 1u voxels as teeth: instead every footprint triangle is the patch, its rock share fading in over 2.5u under the ground, and the walls past the footprint use the same rock formula as the variant (tunnelRock.ts: color from position and normal, the fake sun, the daylight dimming to 6%, the headlamp; vertex wrap, quantization and curvature as the terrain's), so the inner join computes identical colors at shared vertices. The terrain program had one attribute slot left: the patch's rock share and daylight ride one vec2, and the rock color is computed, not carried. No budget for the per-vertex fields: a cave chunk with a mouth ~210ms is accepted.

**Skirts.** Terrain chunk skirts hang 30–1000u below each chunk edge and cut through tunnels. While the player is inside one, every chunk draws a variant that discards skirt pixels (one shared uniform, `uTunnelNoSkirts`, in the terrain's one discard variant, which also carries the LOD cross-fade and the far fade). Seen from outside, through an opening, a chunk's skirt stood in the tunnel's air: the holed variant also drops skirt pixels inside the 20 nearest SKIRT MASKS (capsules over ≤ 24u runs of each path, grown to hold the run's air; one per mouth section and per room). A `discard` costs early depth, which is why the discard variant is picked per chunk and only where a hole or mask touches it (or the chunk needs the LOD or far fade).

**Mouth rocks are the rock dressing's.** Meshed outcrops and boulders (an SDF rock pass) read as blobs and cost a second surface-nets pass per brick. The generator only lists WHERE rocks go (each mouth's `spots`, placed on demand): rim cells found on a 2u grid where the bulged ground crosses into the mouth's ragged opening (`insideMouthOpening`; the footprint capsule the terrain cuts is wider and would line the whole hood with rocks), then strewn rocks. The rock dressing draws low-poly rocks there, with colliders on both sides. Rim rocks floated over the opening on the hood's steep face: rocks sit on the DRAWN ground, none whose footprint reaches over an opening is kept, and a mouth's own rocks are dropped where their footprint rises more than a rock height across.

**Stalactites and stalagmites are rock in the field** (vertical cones, `max(air, −cone)`), so they mesh and collide with the walls for free. A stalactite hung from its own passage's ceiling floated wherever a room or a junction overlapped that passage with a higher ceiling: each formation probes the whole system's field up and down from a point of its passage's air (1u steps, then bisection) and roots 2.5u into the rock it finds (cave generation 19 → 22ms per cell). Stalactite tips keep 3.2u over the floor; stalagmites stand toward a wall. At 1u voxels a tip is blunt: the low-poly look. The player probe leaves them out.

**Sewers are explicit geometry, not an isosurface.** Even snapped onto the exact surface, surface nets drew the sewers organic: wavy walkway edges, lumpy joins, rounded box corners, and plates floating where the field's surface was not where a wall reads. Asked for walls "consistent like the houses", the sewers are BUILT the way buildings are: every street node is a square JUNCTION box (10u, 5.8u tall) or LARGE ROOM (26u, 10u tall, a pool sunk 5u leaving 3.5u walkways: the player is 2u, it swims); pipes are one profile (walkways 16u under the street, a 4u cylinder whose axis stands 1.2u over them, cut flat at them, a 2.8u channel 1.2u deep) extruded wall to wall in ≤ 4u slices, their floor flat across each box and sloping between the walls; a box wall around a pipe's arch, a doorway, or (in the ceiling) a manhole's circle is filled by quads between rays from the hole's center; a box's floor is a grid cut at the channel and pool edges, each cell at its level, with a plumb face wherever two levels meet. Faces are level, plumb or arch only (a test pins it). Each triangle belongs to the chunk holding its centroid; the colliders are the same triangles. Manholes come down into junctions over a floor corner (2.3u in from both walls), never into a pipe. Two systems of one district can share streets: their nodes are merged by key (links OR'd, a manhole kept, room wins, the first door kept) and their pipes by endpoints, in key order, and a door is dropped where the merge put a pipe or a room behind it; the plates are laid out from the MERGED layout, so a merge never leaves one in a room or over an opening. A side room is placed only where sampling its floor plan (every 1.75u, at foot, waist and head height) finds no other air within 0.3u: a radius clearance around each piece rejected every door once junction boxes stood next to them.

**The channel's air runs past each node.** Walking to the end of a channel (a dead end's middle, a corner's middle square) teleported the player to the street: the pipe's channel in the field stopped at the node, while the drawn trench runs on by its half width, so the probe called that stretch rock, the player "outside", and the surface net lifted them out. The channel (and its liquid) run 1.4u past both ends; a test pins that every drawn floor point reads as air 0.3u up.

**Plates like windows; darker walls; taller doors; manholes from both sides.** The shader-drawn plate grid read as a busy texture: walls are plain metal (linear 0.08/0.105/0.15, darkened twice so the plates read against them), and the odd bolted plate is an OBJECT, laid out in ROWS sharing a look (0.8–2.4 × 0.6–1.8u, a leaning top edge at 30%, round at 15%, one of ten steel blues/grays), each plate a little off it, rows seeded along the pipes and over every box wall clear of its openings. On a pipe's arch a flat plate stands off the curve by its sagitta h²/8R, so arch plates stay ≤ 1.1u tall and sit half a sagitta in. Doors are 2 × 3.5u (homes: 2.6 × 3.84), side rooms 7u square and 4.6u tall. Every second node of the main path gets a manhole (one or two per system made them hard to find; ~3.2 per system now). The ladder runs down to the junction floor (rails continuous), each a collider slab (0.8u wide, 0.2u deep): the player stops against it inside the shaft column, where holding jump climbs; its face stands 0.98u from the shaft center, clear of the 0.5u capsule a manhole drops down the middle. From above, the click test is the COVER wherever it lies (in the hole or set aside), not the hole; from below it is the hole up the shaft. The toggle records the new state before the cover moves, and the slide starts from wherever the cover is NOW (a slide reading its start from the state began at its end: the cover jumped). Anything standing in a tunnel (liquid, plates, doors, rungs) is lit as the walls are (no daylight, the headlamp): under the scene's sun they stood out bright in the dark. Material colors are sRGB and the walls' vertex colors linear: the doors' and plates' hexes sit well above the walls' to read as the same metal.

**A manhole frame; the cover and frame follow the DRAWN street.** A thin ring showed around an open hole: the shaft wall is cut against the ground as LOD1 draws it, and the nearer LOD drew the street a little lower, so the wall poked through as a line. An iron FRAME (0.85–1.55u, 0.04u thick, with an underside: looking up a closed shaft, the sky showed in a ring without one) covers both that line and the terrain hole's edge, and the closed cover rests on it. City streets are not flat: across the 3.1u frame the ground varied by up to 0.27u, so a flat frame sank up to 0.21u into it and a flat cover clipped through, closed or set aside. The worker samples the ground under each manhole (24 angles × the inner and outer radius, the HIGHER of the drawn and the exact ground, since the player always stands on LOD1) and the client drapes the frame on it (its top 0.06u over the ground, its feet 0.05u under); each cover rest (on the frame, or aside on the street 2.75u from the hole) is a rigid disc tilted to the best-fit plane of 25 points under it and raised until none is closer than 0.04u (the terrain shader snaps vertices to 0.025u; 0.02 was not enough). Over 320 rests sampled at 144 points each, the closest underside was 0.024u clear; the steepest tilt was 9.3°. The slide interpolates the tilt as well as the position.

**Cave LOD: the entrance stands on LOD1 ground, a far mouth is an impostor.** The caves drew to a hand-picked 600u while the patch and walls stand on the ground as LOD1 draws it, and nothing marked a mouth past that. Approaching, a mesh flashed before the cave rendered (a chunk loads by its 256u bounds, so its entrance appeared while the terrain under the mouth was still LOD2, and the patch floated or sank against it; an entrance spanning two chunks also showed half of itself). Now an entrance shows only once every chunk its footprint spans is loaded (the worker answers for every such chunk, even an empty one), and only a mouth whose chunks are all loaded is a LOD1 SITE: the terrain draws the LOD1 chunks within 24u of it at LOD1 out to 840u (1–2 extra LOD1 chunks per mouth in reach, each with its collider), and the entrance's fade is the LOD1 swap's own progress under it (`lod1ShareAt`), never faster than a swap: the ground change and the entrance happen together, by complementary screen-door pixels against the terrain's own ground (the cut discards only the pixels the patch draws), as the terrain's LOD swaps do. A plain alpha fade was rejected: the patch and walls are opaque and depth-written, sorted with the terrain. Cave chunks load a chunk ahead (840 + 256u; a dressing chunk loads when its CENTRE is within its distance, so a mouth near a chunk's far edge sat on LOD1 ground ~180u before its chunk loaded), every wall and patch invisible until a mouth of theirs fades in, so the early chunks cost generation and memory but no draws. Past that a mouth is an IMPOSTOR: a dark disc the opening's size standing in the opening and facing out, one instance per mouth, the same at every LOD, pulled toward the camera (0.6% of its distance, 0.5–12u) so coarse ground in front of an opening it does not model never covers it; drawn only to 1260u (0.75 × LOD2's ring), dithering out from 1008u; 75% opaque, blended, no depth write; centred on the tube's axis (0.7 radii over the floor point: centred there it sat visibly under the real hole); spreading 1.35 opening radii and fading from a solid core (0.35 radii, set 0.12 radii up: the deepest shadow sits under the hood) to a lighter rim, so it blends into the slope with a sense of depth. Rejected: a spot in the terrain shader (it was only ever as good as the ground's LOD: it shrank and grew as the ground under it changed LOD), shrinking the spot, a middle stage (the far spot on LOD1 ground read as a hole too big for the cave), an entrance mesh fitted to LOD2's grid (a second drawn-ground model).

**The snow is one DRIFT sheet.** Asked for touching piles to merge into one snow mesh: `snow/snowDrift.ts` lays ONE continuous sheet per 256u dressing chunk over every `snow: true` biome (a spec flag carried in the domain config, like `caves`, so the server knows it), on the ground as LOD1 draws it. Where snow lies is an AMOUNT over a threshold (the rule in snow-drift/README.md); the sheet is cut along that threshold's smooth curve at the ground and rises over a quarter-circle edge to the pile's depth. A sheet crossing the ground by itself drew spiky slivers, and one plunging cell by cell drew the 2u grid's steps. Its slope is read across a whole LOD1 cell (across 1u it jumped at every triangle edge, and the outline zigzagged). A snow line (none below 40u, full by 100u) because patches of piles on low ground read as stray scraps on ground already snow-colored. Asked taller as long as it never tops the player (2u): depth 0.6 + 0.8 drift, up to 1.5× high up, capped at 1.85u (mean depth 1.14u at 80–160u ground height, 1.43u above; deepest waded at ~0.59 of walking speed). Wading slows by 0.22 per unit of depth, to 0.35 at the least, inside `stepCharacter`, so the player and the server's NPCs alike (0.5µs per step warm, 2.4µs filling its cover lattice). Coverage by height (mountain + tundra, before the snow line): 36% below 20u, 19% at 20–80u (steep flanks), 29% at 80–160u, 61% above. Far snow is the TERRAIN's color, not a sheet: a sheet 0.4–1.5u over the ground on the 17.5u+ far grids flickered and slipped through the slopes (at kilometres the depth buffer cannot part two surfaces a unit apart); the terrain worker's `snowDepths` ride the `underwaterDepth` attribute NEGATIVE (snow never lies under water, and the terrain program has no attribute slot left) and the terrain whitens by them, at EVERY LOD (on far chunks only, the snow vanished between a chunk turning LOD1 and its piles streaming in): the piles stand on that near, so they only add a light dusting at their edges. The ground's own snow reaches 15° steeper and a little past the piles' edge, in the snow region's drift-varied white, fading into the rock gradually (asked: the edge was hard).

**Rocks on the steep slopes (asked).** Fewer in the valleys and the tundra, more on the mountain's steep ground: a field keeps a flat point with `flatKeep` (mountain 0.12, tundra 0.3), every point by `fullDeg` (32°/25°); the mountain's density rose 380 → 900 so its slopes gain. Biggest rock 4.5 → 13.5u (3×, still rare: sizes are cubically biased small). Their look is the mountain wall's rock (texture, triplanar, tinted) in the terrain's own light: under the scene's lights they read shiny. One merged mesh per chunk: a billboard far tier never matched their facets, texture and light, and a lump is only 20 triangles.

**Bumpier mountains (asked: caves looked out of place on the smooth slopes).** The main noise is squared and faded to 25% at the foot, where the caves are, so it cannot carry small bumps. A biome's noise now takes `detail` LAYERS added linearly at every depth (noise.ts `biomeNoiseHeight`): the mountain has broad smooth bulges (~400/200u, ±45u) and bumps (~150–19u, ±22u), and its main noise went to 4 octaves at persistence 0.6. Heights moved, so caves, rocks and snow moved with them, once. Jaggedness on the steep faces was asked for, then withdrawn.

**Door and manhole state is client-local and lasts the session** (a closed manhole still lights its shaft: the light is baked). Door clicks keep their two owners (buildings on their own listener and the server's sync; the sewers on a window listener while a target is hovered). One `<Rocks/>` mount covers the tundra. The server has no tunnels (known, accepted: no tunnel colliders, manholes or doors; an NPC walks over cave air and a shaft as solid ground; nothing keeps spawned actors off cave openings).

## Rejected and superseded (kept so they are not tried again)

**Snow on the rim; rim piles; a snow collar; a solid roll; one swept lip profile; a rock lip and band; the lip as a rounded ridge** — every cover for the line where a heightfield's per-pixel hole met a separate wall mesh, all superseded by "Entrances are ONE surface". Soft snow piles lining the rim every ~2u leaned a third of their size out over the edge (walked through); rows of separate lumps read as beads, so each mouth got ONE draped blanket (dressing/cave-snow), deepest at the rim, a rounded lip hanging 1.2u into the opening, its outer edge tucked into the ground, triangles clipped where the lip ends; from inside the mouth its edges showed as layered paper, so the rim became a closed TUBE of snow (120 stations, 12 sides, radius ~1.5u ±30%); the tube sat on the blanket like a separate sausage, so the rim became a swept LIP PROFILE rounding a nose into the opening and curling back under, run around the whole rim (0.45 the size across the floor); draped on the exact ground it floated in shards on the hood, so it drape on the drawn ground. Asked to remove the snow around the mouths: the collar (and its server soft spots) went; a terrain-side rock BAND (2.5u around the cut, in the holed variant) and a LIP of wall rock (a ribbon from 1.3u outside the cut, over the edge, down into the opening and back out 0.9u under the ground) replaced it; lying on the ground the lip read as a frame, so it ran 0.06–0.2u under the drawn ground; a hairline still showed, so it became a rounded ridge cresting 0.26u over the ground; the ridge looked worse. All deleted with the one-surface rewrite.

**Soft (slowing) colliders.** The snow collar's soft spots were sensor boxes every capsule walked through at a fraction of its speed (`DressingColliderPart.slowing`, `markSlowing`, on both client and server). With the collar gone the plumbing had no user and was removed; wading is `snowSpeedFactor` alone.

**The shaft rim; the ground cut splits by horizontal extent (Oct 2026).** Looking up a manhole shaft, the sky showed in slivers around the ceiling's hole and, after the rings were made one, in specks at its vertices. Two causes. The shaft wall's ring was laid in WORLD axes while the ceiling's hole ring (`shaftOutline`) is in the box frame: rotated against each other by the district's angle, two sixteen-gons on one circle. The shaft wall now stands on the hole's own ring points.
