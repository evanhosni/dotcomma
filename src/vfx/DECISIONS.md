# Lighting and VFX — decisions

> Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md (Oct 2026) to keep it under the context limit. Section names quoted in the text ("Blending", "Rivers", …) refer to the other decision docs listed in CLAUDE.md under "Where the docs are".

**Lighting and VFX**
- **Lamp glow uses NO real point lights** (`lighting/lampGlow.ts`): a data texture of one head per 24u cell, sampled 3×3 per fragment — constant cost for any lamp count, and it lights the UNLIT terrain. A pool of real nearest-N lights was tried and scrapped: lights visibly ignite on approach. If a lamp is rendered, its light is rendered.
- **World curvature is a VERTEX effect, never post-processing** (`vfx/curvature.ts`): bending the finished image moves pixels without moving depth, so occlusion, parallax and the horizon would disagree with the geometry. It is purely visual — physics, raycasts, placement and the height pipeline stay FLAT, and every interaction reach (doors 6u, CRT 14u) sits inside the flat zone. It works on the camera-relative VIEW position (never an absolute world coordinate) after quantization, and is applied from exactly FIVE places: the terrain vertex shader, the water shader, the foliage shader, `prepareActorMaterial`, `prepareChunkMaterial`. The sky is exempt (a dome pinned to the camera); frustum culling uses UNBENT bounds; there are no shadow casters, so no depth-pass patch exists.
- **The spawn fade is a screen door on material TWINS** (`vfx/spawnFade.ts`): materials are shared (one exterior material for every building), and three uploads a material's uniforms only when the material changes between draws, so a per-object uniform on a shared material cannot work; a fading object draws with `Object.create(base)` twins carrying their own `uSpawnFade` and reading everything else (the program cache key included — no new programs) through to the base. HAZARD: a twin must never be re-patched or have `needsUpdate` set (that forks its version from the base). GLTF clones are opaque for good (the old 1s alpha fade forced `transparent` on every clone material).
- **The nightfall hitch** (`sky/DayNightCycle.tsx`): the moon and stars first render at the exact frame dusk begins, and the lazy compile+link there (expensive under Windows/ANGLE) was a visible hitch, so the cycle `gl.compile()`s the SCENE (deferred one frame) at mount. Compiling only the celestial GROUP does NOT work: light counts are part of the program key, and a lightless subtree compiles programs the real render never reuses. `gl.compile` uses `traverseVisible`, so the day-invisible moon/stars are flipped visible for the call, and a 2-frame warm draw (moon at scale 0.001, stars at opacity 0) uploads their buffers. A post-load scene-wide re-compile was tried and REMOVED: it was itself a big synchronous hitch tens of seconds into play (streamed content is covered by `warmPrograms` / `uploadOnFirstDraw` instead).
- **The city lights are a FIXED pool of parked point lights** (`CityLights`, `world/…/city/lights.tsx`, one `cityLights` driver however many biomes mount it): a changing scene light count recompiles every lit material. Only lit materials and the terrain's point-light loop receive them.
- **Underwater is a full-screen pass, not a per-material fog** (`vfx/underwater.ts`, Oct 2026): blur needs the finished image, and a pass reading the depth buffer fogs every object class with no patch at the five curvature sites. It runs only while the camera is under water. The scene renders into a target flagged `isXRRenderTarget` with an sRGB texture: three keys a program by the target's output color space and tone mapping, so a plain target would have linked a SECOND program for every material on the first dive (the terrain's alone takes ~1s under ANGLE). Flagged, the programs are the screen's own, and the sRGB8 storage hands the pass back exactly the screen's values (the custom shaders write display values, the lit ones encode). Verified headless: fog, blur and seaweed draw (program links on the dive not yet measured).
- **Lights fade instead of switching** (`lighting/lampGlow.ts`, `CityLights`): a lamp head fades in/out over `LAMP_FADE_SECONDS` (the fade rides in the grid texel's w under the next color's threshold, so the color select is untouched); a city light fades by its distance margin to the first site outside the pool, so a re-pick only ever swaps a light at ~0 weight; an aura sprite fades by its VIEW DEPTH, because a screen-aligned sprite is clipped whole when its center crosses the camera's view plane.

- **Quantization grows with distance (Nov 2026).** Evan asked for vertex snapping that coarsens with distance, with no max distance and strong enough to read on far terrain. Past `start` the grid is `size × ((d − start) / 1km)^power`, rounded DOWN to a fixed step list, never under the base grid. First cut was linear from 200u (0.025u per u: 4u at 420u); Evan wanted NOTHING on the player's vicinity and much more on the far rings, so the overworld's curve is `{ start: 700, size: 12, power: 2 }`: untouched across the LOD1 ring (420u) and the detailed buildings (625u), 1u at 1km, 4u at 1.5km, 15u at 2km, 60u at 3km, 120u at 4km, 210u at 5km, 420u at 7km. Three devmode sliders (start, size, power). Then turned OFF by default the same day (Evan): no domain sets `quantizationGrowth`, the uniform's size is 0 and its start/power keep the tuned 700/2, so the devmode size slider alone brings it back (12 = the curve above). Steps, not a continuous size: a grid sized by the exact distance re-snaps every vertex on every camera move (the whole world swims); with steps, only the vertices crossing a band jump. Every step DIVIDES `WORLD_WRAP` (asserted at load): the terrain, water and tunnels anchor the lattice to WRAPPED coordinates and the patched materials to absolute ones, and only a divisor makes those the same lattice, so the ground and what stands on it snap together, with no seam at a wrap line. The distance is that of the UNQUANTIZED view position, so a vertex shared by two meshes (chunk edges, the sewers' duplicated join points) gets the same grid in each. Still applied ONCE, in `QUANTIZE_GLSL`, so every call site inherits it; the sprite tier stays exempt.

- **Two dream effects, the imitation and the real one (Nov 2026).** Evan asked for the early-AI "Google dream" look, then for the actual technique. `vfx/dream.ts` imitates it in one shader (nearly free); `vfx/deep-dream/` runs real DeepDream. A full DeepDream is dozens of forward + backward passes per image (~a second on a GPU), so it can never be per-frame: it is made PROGRESSIVE (2 gradient steps per worker request, each request starting from the previous dream reprojected through the scene depth onto the current camera, 92% dream + 8% fresh scene), which is also what keeps the patterns stuck to surfaces while the camera moves, as optical flow did in Google's dream videos. The network is MobileNet v1 1.0 (tfjs layers format, 17MB): it is a LINEAR chain of conv → batch norm → relu6 blocks, so applying them in order eagerly runs it at any resolution, where the saved model's input is fixed at 224². Inception (the original's net) is ~90MB. `conv_pw_10` grows fur/feather textures, `conv_pw_12` grows dog heads and legs (chosen). PRESENTATION: the dream ITSELF over the scene (`mix(scene, dream, strength × coverage)`). REJECTED by Evan: showing only what the network added (scene + (dream − the scene at the feed's resolution)), and any blend that keeps the scene readable — the effect lost its intensity; at full strength he wants to be unable to tell a beeble from a building. Step size, steps, feedback and layer are his look: change them only when he asks. OVERSCAN: 1.6 rejected (the dream got visibly coarser on screen), 1.2 chosen; the border fade is DERIVED from it (`(1 − 1/overscan) / 2`, exactly the margin beyond the screen), so a still camera shows none of it (computed fade at every edge pixel centre: 1.0) and only a turn brings the soft edge on screen. A hand-set 0.12 band was wider than the 1.2 margin (0.083) and dimmed the still screen's edges to ~0.92. A DISCONTINUITY in the feed at the screen's edge gets dreamed into a BORDER fixed on screen: off-screen texels took the pure previous dream and on-screen ones 8% fresh scene, so every request handed the network a seam exactly there (measured on a still screenshot, sum |rgb − the strip 12–28px in| over the outermost px: 49–187 vs 2–9 inside, a 3px band); one rule for every texel, with the edge's colour AND depth clamped outward, took it to 4–19 with no band.
  - MEASURED (Nov 5 2026; headless, GTX 980, d3d11, city address, 60s from setting the slider; Firefox 158 over WebDriver BiDi, Chrome over CDP). The start-up drops were NOT main-thread work (no long tasks during the hitch in Chrome) but the WORKER'S SHADER LINKS: every WebGL context in the browser shares one GPU-process thread, so while it linked one of tfjs's programs the game's frame waited (the worst game frames matched the worst single links, up to ~0.9s, one by one). Before → after, Firefox: ready 16.6s → 4.3s, worst frame 854 → 97ms, frames > 50ms 43 → 9, time in them 8.7s → 0.7s; steady 65 fps (p95 42ms, 44 frames > 50ms per 40s) vs 113 off → 113 fps (p95 14ms, none) vs 132 off. Chrome: ready 15.2s → 1.6s, worst 903 → 104ms, > 50ms 45 → 4 (9.8s → 0.3s); steady 124 → 144 fps (= off). A request (2 steps) 117 → 42ms in Firefox, 86 → 25ms in Chrome; tfjs programs 176 + ~96 more over the first dreams → 87, flat; tensors flat over 2,500 requests. What did it:
  - The blocks are run from their WEIGHTS, not tfjs-layers: its convolutions are `fused.conv2d`, whose custom gradient computes the FILTER gradient every step (18 Conv2DDerFilter kernels, 1.9s of Firefox linking, their GPU time thrown away); a 1×1 conv's input gradient was `Conv2DDerInputPacked`, a shader looping 3× over all output channels that the D3D compiler took up to 0.95s to link, 12 of them (6.1s); batch norm cost 20 kernels. Now each 1×1 conv is a matMul (its gradient a matMul), batch norm is FOLDED into the conv's weights and a bias once, and the first conv's input gradient uses the unpacked shader (`WEBGL_PACK_CONV2DTRANSPOSE` off: the packed one linked in ~250ms). Checked against the layers path on a random image: activations equal to 3·10⁻⁶ relative, the gradient's mean difference 0.007% of its mean.
  - The jitter's ROLL is a gather: a concat of two slices bakes the split into its shader, so every new jitter value linked new programs (~96 more over the first ~7s of dreaming, each a 50–75ms game frame).
  - With KHR_parallel_shader_compile (Chrome) every kernel is compiled once in tfjs's compile-only mode and polled (`ENGINE_COMPILE_ONLY` + `checkCompileCompletionAsync`): it links off that shared thread (4.3 → 1.6s, 14 → 4 frames > 50ms). Firefox has no such extension. REJECTED there: holding the compile/link calls back and replaying them one program per rest as long as its link (≤ half that thread): worst frame unchanged (104ms: the biggest single link), frames > 50ms 10 → 15, warm-up 4.9 → 6.1s — pacing cannot split a link, only fewer or smaller programs help.
  - Only the shards of the blocks up to the layer are fetched (`tf.io.loadWeights` from the manifest): 9.5 of 17MB+, the last block and the classifier are never run.
  - Remaining (Firefox): ~3.5s of ~55 fps while the ~87 kernels link (programs of 30–100ms each: matMul, depthwise input gradient, mean). The worker now dreams ~25–30 requests/s on this GPU (it starts the next as soon as the last returns); pacing requests (a minimum interval) would buy back the remaining steady-state gap at the price of slower growth: not done, the growth rate is part of the look.
  - HAZARD: tfjs's WebGL backend compiles its kernels per TENSOR SHAPE; a 320×158 request after a 64×36 warm-up recompiled hundreds of programs and stalled the worker for 30s+ (and slowed the game, sharing the GPU process). The feed size is therefore FIXED whatever the screen's aspect (the dream is mapped through its camera's projection, so it needs none), `WEBGL_USE_SHAPES_UNIFORMS` is on, and the worker warms at the real size.
  - PRELOAD (Nov 6 2026; Firefox 158 headless over BiDi, d3d11, /snow/mountain, slider to 1 at ~45s). Before, the worker started on the first frame the effect was on: turning it on gave 4s at 78–92 fps, 7 frames > 50ms (worst 90ms; Chrome: 1 frame of 63ms). Now it starts 4s after load: turning it on gives 0 frames > 50ms (worst 21ms, 131 fps steady), and the links land at boot (10–15s after load: 11 frames > 50ms, worst 97ms) instead. The price is tfjs + 8.7MB of weights downloaded by every visitor.
- **The post effects are ONE chain** (`vfx/screenPass.ts`): underwater, trace, deep dream and dream each take the previous pass's color and the scene's depth, so any combination stacks; with none active the scene draws straight to the screen, exactly as before the chain existed.

- **The trace is screen-space, NEVER reprojected (Nov 2026).** Evan asked for the frozen-desktop window trail (a dragged window leaving a stack of copies). In a 3D view every pixel is redrawn each frame, so "what stays behind" needs a rule: a stamp keeps a pixel only where it is NEARER than the live frame (`trace.ts`, distance in the history's alpha). Reprojecting the history would pin copies to the world, which is a ghost, not a trail: the copies must stay where they were drawn ON SCREEN. Stamps are discrete (`interval`, 0.05s) so the copies read as separate windows instead of a smear at high fps. Copies RECEDE multiplicatively (scale-free across near and far) rather than fading their color, so they are always crisp and are overtaken from behind, like windows covered by newer ones; the sky (60000u) never covers one. Verified headless (d3d11, /snow/mountain, camera turning 1.2 rad/s at strength 1): crisp stacked copies of the snow ridges, no console errors.
